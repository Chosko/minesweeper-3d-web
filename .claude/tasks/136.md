# Task 136 — 2D tile art parity with 3D

Target: claude

## Goal
Make the Classic 2D board look like the same game as the 3D one. This is row
W-10 of `docs/UX_REVIEW.md` § Approved scope and work plan (Area: Classic 2D,
Size: Medium, Phase 2), and it closes P-63 (Minor): the 2D board looks flat
beside the 3D one, with plain tiles that lack the bevel, highlight and border
of the cube faces, and numbers, flags and mines drawn in a different style.
The approved solution, verbatim: "Draw the 2D tile from the same recipe as a
cube face (bevel, highlight, border) at tile size, and reuse the 3D number,
flag and mine artwork so both modes read as one game, in both themes." The
work serves every Classic 2D player, in play and in the replay viewer, and
the product's identity across modes.

## Acceptance criteria
- **One recipe, two palettes** (P-63): the cube-face drawing in
  `js/textures.js` — closed face (rim, light top-left and dark bottom-right
  bevel, face gradient, gloss highlight), revealed face (rim, edge, shaded
  face), the number glyph (heavy weight, darker same-hue outline, soft drop
  shadow, centred on its glyph box), the mine (eight spikes, body,
  highlight), the flag (base, pole, shaded cloth) and the wrong-flag cross —
  moves into one shared module, `js/tile-art.js`, that draws at any size and
  takes every colour as an argument. It imports neither three.js nor
  `js/tokens.js` and reads no token. `js/textures.js` draws its atlas
  through it with its own literal palette. `js/classic2d/tile-skin.js`
  draws every 2D tile through it with colours read from tokens.
- **3D unchanged**: every atlas layer `drawLayer` produces (closed, flag,
  mine, exploded mine, numbers 0–26, wrong flag) is pixel-identical to the
  drawing before this change, checked in the browser against digests
  recorded before the refactor. The 3D palette stays unthemed: the "3D scene
  is not themed" checks in `tests/tokens.test.mjs` and
  `tests/theme.test.mjs` also cover `js/tile-art.js`.
- **2D states keep their meaning** (P-63, in both themes): closed is the
  raised cube face; flagged is the closed face with the 3D flag; revealed is
  the revealed face, with the 3D number glyph in `--color-number-<n>` and no
  glyph for zero; mine is the revealed face with the 3D mine; exploded is the
  mine on the exploded face; wrong flag is the revealed face with the 3D flag
  under the 3D cross; pressed is a sunken face (see Decisions), distinct from
  both closed and revealed. The placeholder for an unknown state or a number
  outside 0–8 stays visibly wrong. `TILE_STATES`, `paintTile`,
  `paintTileWith`, `createTileSkin` and `drawTile` keep their signatures.
- **Number colours keep their meaning**: the 2D number tokens 1–8 keep
  their hue per theme. Each reaches at least 4.5:1 against every point of
  its theme's revealed face (both ends of the face shading), checked in
  `tests/tokens.test.mjs`. A value moves only as far as that requires,
  keeping its hue.
- **Tokens, no second palette**: every 2D colour the recipe needs beyond the
  existing tile tokens (rim, bevel light and dark, face shading, gloss,
  revealed edge and shade, the mine highlight, the flag cloth shading, the
  wrong-flag cross outline) is a new `--color-tile-*` token in
  `css/tokens.css` with a light and a dark value. The light values match the
  3D palette so light 2D and 3D look alike; the dark values are drawn for
  the dark theme. `js/classic2d/tile-skin.js` still holds no literal colour.
  Closed and revealed stay distinguishable by more than colour (relief and
  border) in both themes.
- **Legible at the floor**: every state and number 1–8 is legible at 26 px
  tiles (the floor of decision 10, set by task 134) and at `MIN_TILE_SIZE`,
  in both themes and at device pixel ratios 1 and 2. The recipe's
  proportions scale with tile size, and every rim, border and bevel band is
  at least one device pixel wide, so the relief survives at the floor. The
  browser legibility check in `tests/tile-skin.test.mjs` runs at both sizes
  and passes, with screenshots of every state.
- **Tile cache and per-cell redraw stay**: the cache still pre-renders every
  state and number once per tile size, pixel ratio and theme, and drops on a
  theme change; a full redraw is still image copies, and a cell change still
  redraws that cell only.
- **Tests first**: `tests/tile-skin.test.mjs` replaces "the tile is drawn
  plainly: no gradients, shadows or patterns" with tests that pin the
  recipe — each state draws through the shared module with its own tokens,
  closed and flagged carry the bevel, the wrong flag draws the flag and the
  cross — and pins the cache, the placeholder and the 26 px legibility
  check. `tests/tokens.test.mjs` pins the new tokens in both themes and the
  number contrast against the shaded face. The "3D scene is not themed"
  checks cover the shared module.
- **What not to break** (`docs/UX_REVIEW.md` § Decisions taken, "What not to
  break"): Classic 2D input, hit-testing and layout are untouched, and the
  3D board looks exactly as before.
- `.claude/domain/features/square-tile-skin.md` states the agreed design
  change — the 2D tile is drawn from the cube-face recipe with the 3D
  number, flag and mine artwork in both themes, from one shared recipe
  module — in every passage that stated the old one, and settles the points
  listed under Decisions. Nothing else in the document changes.

## Decisions
- **Design change agreed (user decision, 2026-10-12):** settled by prior
  instruction, P-63 of `docs/UX_REVIEW.md`, approved without condition under
  § Approved scope and work plan (row W-10).
  - `square-tile-skin.md` § Scope and non-goals, "Low-fidelity drawing"
    (every state a flat fill, simple edge and plain glyph): each state is
    drawn from the cube-face recipe (bevel, highlight, border) with the 3D
    number, flag and mine artwork, in both themes.
  - `square-tile-skin.md` § Scope and non-goals, non-goal "High-fidelity
    visual dressing — a later pass": the cube-face dressing is in scope now.

  Settled here, without conflict: § Architecture, "Tile painter", draws
  through a shared recipe module that also draws the 3D atlas, while the 3D
  tiles and palette stay as they are (§ Non-goals, "The 3D cube tiles");
  § Interfaces, "Contrast contract", measures against every point of the
  shaded revealed face; § Minimum tile size, legibility is checked at the
  26 px floor; the pressed and wrong-flag looks below. This document is
  normally `/architect`'s; this task updates it for exactly this and for
  nothing else. A further design decision met at implementation time is not
  covered: leave that passage untouched and name it under *Follow-ups* as a
  precise follow-up.
- **One shared module, colours passed in**: reuse means one drawing, not a
  copy, so the two modes cannot drift apart. The 2D skin must stay free of
  three.js (Classic 2D runs without WebGL 2, decision 13) and the 3D palette
  must stay unthemed, so the shared module holds geometry only and each
  caller supplies its palette.
- **Pressed tile**: 3D has no held-press tile, since its `pressed` is the
  engine's revealed flag. The 2D pressed tile is the cube face sunk in:
  bevel inverted (dark top-left, light bottom-right), no gloss, face in
  `--color-tile-pressed`. It reads as "about to open", unlike closed
  (raised) and revealed (flat).
- **Wrong flag shows the flag**: it reuses the 3D wrong-flag tile, a flag
  under a red cross on the revealed face, replacing the 2D crossed mine. The
  meaning stays the same: a flag that was not on a mine.
- **No preconditions on task 134**: the 26 px criterion is checked at 26 px
  directly, so it holds whether or not task 134 has lowered
  `MIN_TILE_SIZE`. Both tasks edit `js/classic2d/tile-skin.js` and
  `tests/tile-skin.test.mjs`; whichever lands second keeps the other's
  changes.
- **Out of scope**: colour-blind-safe number sets (P-57), dropped by
  decision 17 ("Accessibility settings: Not now"); the board scale, tile
  floor, framed panel and cursor ring (W-08, task 134); 2D sound (W-09);
  the 2D end-of-game moment (W-11); any change to the 3D tiles, numbers 9–26
  or the 3D palette.
