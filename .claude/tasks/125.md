# Task 125 — 3D spawn framing and world reference

Target: claude

## Goal
Start every 3D game with the whole box in view from a three-quarter angle,
and give the player a way back to it once they have flown or turned away.
This is row W-14 of `docs/UX_REVIEW.md` § Approved scope and work plan
(Area: 3D, Size: Small, Phase 1). It closes P-24 and P-27 of that document,
under its decision 5 ("Original 3D quirks behind a setting? Fix outright, no
new settings: the spawn pose (P-24) …"). Today the spawn camera sits level
with the top row, close to the face: only four of six rows are on screen,
the top half of the view is sky, the crosshair rests on a top-edge cube, and
the box is seen dead-on, so it reads as a flat wall of tiles with no hint it
is several deep — on every preset and after Play again (P-24, Major, every
game start, worst for first-timers). And there is nothing in the world but
the box: a sky gradient with no horizon or ground, so looking away shows a
flat gradient, eight seconds of S leaves the box 400 units away as a 60 px
blob, and there is no off-screen indicator and no return key (P-27, Major,
first-timers who hold a key, anyone turning at speed). The work serves every
3D player at every start, and anyone who loses the box.

## Acceptance criteria
- **Framed three-quarter spawn** (P-24, decision 5): one pure function
  computes the spawn pose of a box — from its X, Y, Z, the spacing, and the
  camera's vertical field of view and aspect — as a position plus yaw and
  pitch in `FlyCamera`'s convention. The pose looks down onto the box at
  about 15° (the review's "pitch about −15°"; `FlyCamera` pitch + = down)
  and is turned about 20° around the vertical from the original's straight
  view toward +Z, so the near face, the top face and one side face are all
  visible. Both angles are named constants. The ray from the screen centre
  passes through the centre of the near face's centre cube (for an even side,
  the cube at index `floor(side / 2)`), so `pickCell` from the spawn pose at
  the start spacing selects that cube. The distance is the smallest that
  keeps all eight corners of the box inside the view with a named margin
  (about 10 % of the view on each side), horizontally and vertically, at the
  window's current aspect.
- **Every start uses it** (P-24): the first 3D game, Play again, Restart
  from the pause card, the failure offer's Retry and Play a standard board,
  and a start from the debug hook all place the camera through that one
  function; no start path keeps the original pose. It holds for the three
  cube presets and for any custom box from 1 to 100 per side, at wide,
  standard and portrait aspects. There is no setting and no fly-in: the new
  pose replaces the original one (decision 5).
- **Back to start: key** (P-27): during 3D play, while the board accepts
  input (including before the first click, never during the end delay or
  while paused), `Home` and `R` put the camera back to the spawn pose of the
  current box at once, at the current spacing and the current aspect. The
  key does not repeat while held, `preventDefault` stops the browser's own
  Home scrolling while play is active, and it changes nothing else: no
  action is recorded, the clock, flags and view modes are untouched. Both
  keys appear as one row of the 3D keyboard bindings in
  `js/settings/bindings.js`; a hand-written 3D key list still in the markup
  when this lands (the pause card's, which task 119 moves onto the bindings
  source) and the README's 3D controls table get the same row.
- **Back to start: pause card** (P-27): the pause card of a 3D game whose
  board is still in play shows a "Back to start" button after Resume; it
  puts the camera at the spawn pose and resumes exactly as Resume does
  (pointer lock or lockless controller play, by the same source rules). It
  is hidden in Classic 2D and on an ended game, reachable by keyboard and
  controller menu navigation, and Resume keeps the default focus.
- **Recorded as ordinary movement** (P-27): a return to start mid-game shows
  in the replay as the camera's next samples; the replay format, its version
  and the 3D sampler are unchanged. A replay recorded before this change
  plays back its own recorded camera (the original start pose included)
  unchanged, and the recorded games under `tests/fixtures/replays/` still
  verify. The replay viewer's camera before its first sample uses the same
  spawn function.
- **Off-screen arrow** (P-27): during 3D play, when no part of the box's
  bounding box is inside the view frustum, an arrow on the crosshair ring
  points toward the box centre's direction on screen (a centre behind the
  camera points the way the shortest turn goes); it is hidden whenever any
  part of the box is in view, while paused, before play and outside 3D play.
  The in-view test and the arrow's screen angle are a pure function of the
  camera pose, box, spacing, field of view and aspect. The arrow is styled
  like the crosshair (readable over the scene in both themes) and updates in
  the frame loop with the crosshair, so it adds no frames when nothing moves.
- **World reference below the box** (P-27): a faint ground grid lies on a
  horizontal plane a fixed gap below the box's bottom at the current spacing
  (it follows a spacing change), fading toward the sky colour with distance
  so its far edge reads as a horizon line. It is drawn behind every cube,
  writes no depth that could hide a cube, is never pickable, and does not
  change under Shift, Space or Ctrl, the end-of-game effects or the camera
  shake. It is faint but visible against the sky in both themes; the
  renderer still reads no token and subscribes to no theme change.
- **Tests first**: a new unit test pins the spawn function for the three
  presets and customs (1 × 1 × 2, 30 × 16 × 1, 100 × 100 × 100, 2 × 9 × 40)
  at aspects 16:9, 4:3, 21:9 and 9:16 and fields of view 45° and 90°: the
  eight corners project inside the margin, pitch and yaw match the
  constants, the camera lies beyond the planes of the near face, the top
  face and one side face (three faces visible), and `pickCell` from the pose returns the near
  face's centre cube. It also pins the arrow function (hidden with the box in
  view, the angle toward a box to the right, left, above and behind). Shell
  or browser tests pin that `Home` and `R` return the camera during play and
  do nothing while paused or during the end delay, that the pause card's
  "Back to start" shows only for a 3D game in play and resumes, and that the
  bindings row exists; `tests/components.test.mjs`'s debug-hook key list
  follows any rename of `startCameraPos`. A Playwright check screenshots the
  spawn of each cube preset and checks the grid and the arrow by eye.
- **What not to break** (`docs/UX_REVIEW.md` § Decisions taken):
  release-to-act with drag-to-cancel; the reference input conventions —
  0.2°/px look, pitch clamp, WASD/QE at 50 u/s, the forward vector as it is
  today (W-15 owns it), picking from the screen centre, the Shift / Space /
  Ctrl view modes; 3BV, click and efficiency accounting; replays recorded by
  earlier builds.
- `docs/ORIGINAL_SPEC.md` § Controls keeps the original's start pose as the
  reference and states, as a "Web port:" note like the one on `Esc` / `F5`,
  that the port starts framed from the three-quarter view, with `Home` / `R`
  and the pause card's "Back to start" returning there.
- `.claude/domain/product-design.md` states the agreed design change — the
  3D mode keeps its controls and feel except that a game starts framed from
  a three-quarter view instead of the original's pose, and the scene gains a
  return-to-start key and pause action, an off-screen arrow and a faint
  ground grid — in every passage that stated the old one. Nothing else in
  the document changes.

## Decisions
- **Design change agreed (user decision, 2026-10-12):** settled by prior
  instruction — `docs/UX_REVIEW.md` decision 5 (P-24: fix outright, no new
  setting) and P-27, approved without condition under § Approved scope and
  work plan (row W-14).
  - `product-design.md` § Design decisions, "The 3D mode is the existing 3D
    game", says its look, controls and feel are kept; the start pose
    changes from the original's, and the scene gains the return key, the
    pause card action, the off-screen arrow and the ground grid.

  Settled here, without conflict: the ground grid keeps the 3D scene free of
  theme tokens, as `product-design.md` § Visual design system requires.
  `product-design.md` is normally `/product-design`'s; this task updates it
  for exactly this and for nothing else. A further design decision met at
  implementation time is not covered: leave that passage untouched and name
  it under *Follow-ups* as a precise follow-up.
- **The original spec's start pose is changed on purpose** (decision 5).
  `docs/ORIGINAL_SPEC.md` pins "position (0, 10, −8·max), pitch 0, looking
  toward +Z" under Controls (must be preserved). Decision 5 settles that the
  spawn is fixed outright, with no setting to keep the original and without
  the review's fallback of keeping the pose and flying in over 0.8 s. The
  spec keeps describing the original and gains a "Web port:" note; the
  context layer's list of preserved controls drops "start orientation toward
  +Z", and the README's "Re-centred grid" line stops saying the start camera
  lines up with a column of cubes and joins the deliberate changes.
- **Both keys.** The review's "a Home or R key" is read as both: `Home` is
  the conventional key, `R` is free during 3D play (I-06's one-key restart,
  W-29, uses `F2` in play and `R` only on the results screen) and sits under
  the left hand beside WASD. W-24 (keyboard-only 3D, free keys only) must
  treat `R` as taken.
- **No new controller button.** P-27 names a key and a pause card action;
  the pause card is reachable with Start, so controllers return through it.
- **Instant return, current spacing.** The return is a jump, not a flight:
  timed camera flights belong to the deferred camera presets (I-12). The
  spacing is the player's and is kept; the pose is reframed for it, so the
  whole box still fits.
- **Crosshair on a cube.** "Crosshair on the face centre" lands in a gap
  between four cubes on an even side, which selects nothing; aiming at the
  near face's centre cube keeps the spawn's crosshair on a cube.
- **Framing reads the live field of view.** The spawn function takes the
  camera's field of view rather than assuming 45°, so W-15's FOV slider
  (P-32) composes with it without a second change.
- **Grid, not a horizon line alone.** The review offers either; a grid fading
  into the sky gives both the ground and the horizon. It is part of the 3D
  scene wherever the box is drawn (play, the menu backdrop, the replay
  viewer); the off-screen arrow belongs to play only.
- **Boundary with task 120** (row W-02): 120 themes the sky, fog and
  background through a palette the shell hands the renderer. If it has
  landed, the grid takes its colour from that same palette; if not, from the
  fixed sky colour. Either way the renderer stays token-free, so either task
  can land first.
- **Boundary with task 119** (row W-01): 119 replaces the pause card's
  hand-written 3D key list with the bindings component; this task adds its
  row to the bindings source and, while the hand-written list still exists,
  to it too, so either task can land first.
- **Out of scope:** camera presets (I-12, keys 1 to 6 and 0) and snap aiming
  (I-13), deferred by § Approved scope and work plan; a distance limit on
  flight (P-27's problem names it, its approved solution does not); W-15's
  fly-through fade (P-25), forward vector (P-26), spacing (P-30) and FOV
  slider (P-32); W-01's help and hint redesign; W-24's keyboard look and
  actions; W-34's orbit camera.
