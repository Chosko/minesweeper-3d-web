# Task 127 — Right-click on a number

Target: claude

## Goal
Keep the 3D flag-all on a revealed number, but make it recoverable and
visible. This is row W-20 of `docs/UX_REVIEW.md` § Approved scope and work
plan (Area: 3D, Size: Small, Phase 1). It closes P-21 (Major, every 3D
player, worst for newcomers), as settled by decision 3 of § Decisions
taken and under the condition in § Approved scope and work plan: P-21 is
built as undo-last-batch plus visibility, never as a certainty gate.
Today a right-click on a revealed number flags every closed neighbour,
right or wrong: one right-click on a 2 placed 10 flags, and most landed
behind the front face out of sight. A second right-click unflags them all,
hand-placed flags included. Because actions fire on release, a right press
that drifts onto a number while turning does this by accident. After this
task a second right-click on the same number takes back only the flags
that number's flag-all placed, never a hand-placed flag. The affected
cubes flash, the flag sound grows with the count, the mines counter tints
when it goes negative, and the ready card and help say what the action
does.

## Acceptance criteria
- **Rule, as a new rules version** (P-21, decision 3): the 3D profile
  (`PROFILE_3D`) ships version 2 with a new marker for the undo rule (for
  example `revealedUnflag: 'own-batch'`). Its `fidelity` entry cites the
  new deliberate difference in `.claude/domain/features/3d-board-graph.md`.
  Version 2 becomes current, so every new 3D game plays it. Version 1 stays
  in `PROFILES` unchanged and keeps unflag-all. Its tests keep passing, and
  every 3D replay recorded under it, the fixtures under
  `tests/fixtures/replays/` included, still verifies.
- **A flag click on a revealed number under version 2:**
  - When the number has at least one closed, unflagged neighbour, it flags
    every one of them, as today. Each flag it places belongs to that
    number's batch.
  - When every closed neighbour is already flagged, it unflags exactly the
    flagged neighbours that belong to that number's batch, and that batch
    is then empty. Hand-placed flags and flags another number's flag-all
    placed stay. When the batch is empty, nothing changes; the click is
    still recorded and counted as one wasted flag click.
  - Unflagging un-hides neighbours exactly as any unflag does today.
- **The batch through the rest of the game:**
  - *Hand unflag:* a hand unflag removes that cell from the batch, and so
    does an unflag of any other kind. A flag the player later places on
    that cell by hand is hand-placed.
  - *Flag-all over a live batch:* a flag-all on a number whose batch still
    holds flags adds the new ones to that batch. The next undo on that
    number then removes every flag its flag-alls placed since its last
    undo that still stand.
  - *Auto-hide:* when the number hides, its batch is kept as it is. It is
    unreachable while the number is hidden, since hidden cells cannot be
    picked. When an unflag shows the number again, its next right-click
    applies to that batch. A number only hides when its flags match it
    exactly with every other neighbour open, so a hidden number's batch
    holds only mines.
  - *Before the first click:* flags placed before the first reveal are
    hand-placed, and so are the flags the session replays onto the
    arriving board.
  - *Loss:* after a loss nothing applies, as today.
  - *Snapshot and restore:* `snapshot()` carries every flag's batch
    membership, and `restore()` sets it back. A restored game then behaves
    exactly like the game that took the snapshot.
  - *Replay:* batch membership depends only on the board and the action
    stream under the recorded rules version. It is never recorded in the
    replay. The replay format, its version and the final-state digest do
    not change. A 3D game recorded under version 2 whose stream holds a
    flag-all, a hand unflag of one batch cell and an undo verifies, and
    seeking back and forth across the undo shows the same flags as live
    play.
- **Click and 3BV accounting unchanged in kind:** a flag click on a number
  is one flag click however many cells it changes. It is effective when it
  places a flag and wasted otherwise. 3BV and efficiency are unaffected.
- **Memory:** batch membership is allocated once per game, never per
  action, so the existing "mass flag and unflag allocate no per-cell
  memory" check still holds. The measured large-board bytes a cell
  (`tests/engine-3d-view.test.mjs`) are updated to the new figure, and
  stated wherever the old one is stated.
- **Flash:** when a flag click on a revealed number changes flags in live
  play, every cube it flagged or unflagged plays a short flash (duration
  and peak are named constants, well under a second). The flash shows
  even when the cube sits behind other cubes from the camera, so a batch
  behind the front face is seen. A hand flag on a closed cube does not
  flash. `isAnimating()` is true during a flash and false after it, so
  render-on-demand draws it and then stops. The per-frame cost is bounded
  by the number of flashing cubes, never O(n) in the board. `setGame`, a
  restart and the end of the game drop running flashes. The 3D scene still
  reads no theme token. The replay viewer shows no flash, as it plays no
  sound.
- **Flag sound scales with the count:** `Sfx.flag(on, count)` draws its
  voice from a pure, exported function of `(on, count)`. It is
  non-decreasing in steps and level as count grows, capped at the
  26-neighbour maximum. Counts 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 sound distinct. Count 1
  is today's single flag sound, unchanged. An undo (`on` false, count > 1)
  is the falling mirror of the mass flag. Throttling and the voice cap are
  unchanged.
- **Mines counter tints when negative:** in a running 3D game the HUD's
  mines figure shows in `--color-danger` whenever mines left is below 0,
  and in its normal colour otherwise. This holds in the light and dark
  themes, at a contrast of at least 4.5:1 against the HUD background. The
  `negative` class and its CSS rule exist today; a browser check confirms
  the computed colour in the running game and fixes whatever keeps it
  from showing.
- **Ready card and help copy** (P-21):
  - The 3D ready card's keys gain the line "**Right** on a number flags
    all its closed cubes · **Right** again takes back only those".
  - In the bindings source (`js/settings/bindings.js`), the 3D mouse row
    for Right click reads "Flag / unflag. On a number: flag all its closed
    neighbours; right-click it again to take back only those flags".
  - The controller list gains the same sentence for `{lt}` on a number.
  - Every help surface that shows the 3D bindings (pause card, Controls
    panel, H panel) shows the new rows. README.md's 3D controls row for
    Right click says the same.
- **Tests first:**
  - Engine unit tests pin, under version 2: flag-all, undo of the own
    batch only, with hand-placed flags and another number's flags kept;
    a hand unflag leaving the batch; flag-all over a live batch; the batch
    kept through an auto-hide and usable once the number is shown again;
    an empty-batch click recorded as one wasted flag click; snapshot and
    restore round-tripping membership. Version 1 still unflags all.
  - A replay test records a version 2 game with an undo and checks that it
    verifies and that it seeks both ways.
  - The v1 fixtures still verify.
  - A node test pins the sound mapping.
  - A browser test through `__ms` pins the flash (`isAnimating()` true,
    then false), the HUD tint in both themes, and the ready card and
    bindings copy.
  - A Playwright screenshot of a flag-all flashing on Cube Intermediate,
    with part of the batch behind the front face, is checked by eye.
- **What not to break** (`docs/UX_REVIEW.md` § Decisions taken):
  - release-to-act with drag-to-cancel: the accidental drift is answered
    by the undo, not by changing input;
  - the reference input conventions;
  - 3BV, click and efficiency accounting;
  - replays recorded by earlier builds.
- `docs/ORIGINAL_SPEC.md` § Rules keeps the original's `rightClick` rule
  and gains a "Web port:" note, like the one on `Esc` / `F5`, saying that
  a second right-click on a number unflags only the flags its own
  flag-all placed. README.md § Gameplay fidelity lists it as a deliberate
  change.
- `.claude/domain/features/3d-board-graph.md` states the agreed design
  change in every passage that stated the old one, and settles the points
  listed under Decisions. The change: a right-click on a revealed number
  whose closed neighbours are all flagged unflags only the flags that
  number's flag-all placed, never hand-placed ones. Nothing else in the
  document changes.
- `.claude/domain/product-design.md` states the agreed design change in
  every passage that stated the old one: the 3D mode's rules change in a
  third way, a second right-click on a number takes back only the flags
  the first placed. Nothing else in the document changes.

## Decisions
- **Design change agreed (user decision, 2026-10-12):** settled by prior
  instruction. `docs/UX_REVIEW.md` decision 3 says: keep flag-all; a
  second right-click undoes only the flags the previous one added; make
  the action visible. § Approved scope and work plan says P-21 is built
  as undo-last-batch plus visibility, never as a certainty gate.
  - `3d-board-graph.md` § 3D rule profile, "Right-click on a revealed
    cell", says it unflags them all when every one is already flagged.
    Under version 2 it unflags only the number's own batch.
  - `3d-board-graph.md`, the deliberate differences from the original
    game, gains this difference, which follows from decision 3 rather
    than the m2 rules change.
  - `3d-board-graph.md` § Data and state (Game state) and § Large-board
    memory gain batch membership and its bytes a cell.
  - `product-design.md` § Design decisions, "The 3D mode is the existing
    3D game", says its rules change in two ways; a third is added.

  Settled here, without conflict:
  - the batch after a hand unflag, a flag-all over a live batch, an
    auto-hide, a snapshot restore and in a replay, as the criteria state;
  - the rule ships as rules version 2 of the 3D profile;
  - an empty-batch click is one wasted flag click
    (`3d-board-graph.md` § Data and state, Counts).

  `3d-board-graph.md` is normally `/architect`'s and `product-design.md`
  `/product-design`'s; this task updates them for exactly this and for
  nothing else. A further design decision met at implementation time is
  not covered: leave that passage untouched and name it under
  *Follow-ups* as a precise follow-up.
- **No certainty gate, ever** (`docs/UX_REVIEW.md` § Approved scope and
  work plan; P-21's measurement). Do not build a certainty-gated
  anti-chord, nor any gate or check on whether the flags are right. With
  chord it would be a complete single-constraint solver the player could
  drive without reading: blind spamming would win 70 % of no-guess Cube
  Beginner boards and 47 % of Intermediate ones.
- **A new rules version, not an edit to version 1.** The engine's rule
  (`.claude/context/engine.md`, rule profiles) is that a changed rule
  ships as a new version and earlier versions stay playable. Replays
  record their profile version, so version 1 replays keep reproducing
  unflag-all.
- **The batch is membership of standing flags, extended by every flag-all
  since the number's last undo.** "The previous right-click" read
  literally as only the latest flag-all would strand one case. A flag from
  an earlier flag-all of the same number, left standing after a hand
  unflag and a re-flag, could then never be taken back from that number.
  In the common case, one flag-all then one undo, the two readings agree.
- **The undo trigger is the original one**: every closed neighbour
  flagged. Only the set it unflags changes, so the toggle feel is kept.
- **Records are not split.** The board key does not carry the rules
  version, and the change only decides which flags an undo removes. It
  never reveals a cell, so 3BV, clicks and times stay comparable across
  versions.
- **Flash only for a flag click on a number, and shown through
  occluders.** P-21's problem is a batch landing out of sight behind the
  front face; a flash that cubes in front could hide would not answer it.
  A hand flag lands under the crosshair and needs none.
- **No flash or sound in the replay viewer.** They are live-play feedback,
  driven from `onAction`, and the viewer plays no action sounds today.
- **Out of scope:**
  - changing release-to-act or adding a drift guard on the right press
    (§ What not to break);
  - any gate on flag-all;
  - the 3D rules block, toast and hint strips of task 119 (row W-01);
  - everything else in `docs/UX_REVIEW.md`.
- **Boundary with task 119** (row W-01): 119 turns the bindings source
  into the one component the pause card, Controls panel and H panel show,
  and reworks the ready card's lines. The copy here is edited in
  `BINDINGS` and in the ready card's keys. If 119 has not landed, the
  pause card's hard-coded 3D list in `index.html` gets the same sentence.
  Whichever task lands second keeps the other's change and adds its own.
- **Boundary with tasks 125 and 126** (rows W-14, W-19): both edit
  `product-design.md`'s 3D mode bullet, README.md's fidelity list,
  `docs/ORIGINAL_SPEC.md` and `js/render.js` effects (126's pulse). The
  flash and the pulse are separate effects. Whichever lands later keeps
  the earlier changes and adds its own.
