# Task 147 — Touch controls for 3D

Target: claude

## Goal
Make 3D playable by touch on tablets and phones. This is row W-23 of
`docs/UX_REVIEW.md` § Approved scope and work plan (Area: Input, 3D, Size:
Large, Phase 4, after W-22 and W-15). It closes I-14 of that document, which
decision 11 ("Touch a target? Yes") lets proceed. 3D is the product's
identity, and a whole device class is locked out by input alone, while the
menus already lay out well at tablet and phone widths (I-14, tablet
players). After task 142 (W-22, P-39) a coarse-only device sees the 3D ready
card as a notice with one Back action. This task supersedes that notice
there with a real start into touch play. The approved solution, verbatim,
is: "A left-thumb virtual stick to fly, one-finger drag on the right half to
look, tap to reveal, long-press to flag, two-finger tap to chord, pinch for
spacing, a view-modes toolbar with Shift, Space and Ctrl as toggles, a Pause
button above the scene; pointer lock never requested on coarse pointers."
The touch actions feed the existing mouse release and chord state machine,
the same way task 143's keyboard keys do. Task 146's Orbit camera works
under the same gestures. Mouse, keyboard and controller play stay exactly
as they are. The work serves tablet and phone players.

**This is a Large task.** Its acceptance criteria are grouped so the work
can be reviewed in four parts: A movement and look, B actions, C toolbar,
pinch and pause, D orbit. The cross-cutting criteria close the list.

## Acceptance criteria

### A. Movement and look
- **Where touch controls run**: on a coarse-only device (the shell's
  `COARSE_ONLY`, coarse and not fine) the 3D touch controls are mounted for
  3D play. On every other device nothing in this task is mounted or shown,
  and 3D plays as before.
- **A real start** (I-14, superseding task 142's notice there): on a
  coarse-only device the 3D ready card is a button again. It reads "Tap to
  play", lists the touch gestures, and keeps its Back action. A tap enters
  lockless touch play. The controller's Start through `padResume` still
  enters lockless play as task 142 left it.
- **Pointer lock is never requested on a coarse-only device** (I-14, P-39).
  This covers the ready card, resume from the pause card, restart, the
  failed board's Retry and Standard offers, and entering fullscreen. Each
  of them enters or returns to lockless touch play instead.
- **Virtual stick** (I-14): a touch that starts in the left half of the
  scene (below the overlay bar, outside the toolbar) places a stick base
  under the thumb. Dragging deflects it up to a named radius (a constant in
  the new module). The deflection goes through `stickCurve` (deadzone and
  curve) into the camera's analog move, the way the controller's left stick
  does: up is forward, sideways strafes, speed capped at `MOVE_SPEED`, and
  forward follows the view as task 139 leaves it. Lifting the thumb stops
  movement and hides the stick. A tap in the left half acts on nothing.
- **Look drag** (I-14): a one-finger drag that starts in the right half
  turns the camera through `FlyCamera.look` at the mouse's 0.2°/px. Look
  sensitivity and Invert Y apply, and the pitch clamp holds. The stick and
  the look drag work at the same time with two thumbs.
- **Render on demand**: a deflected stick or a moving look drag keeps the
  frame loop drawing, and it idles again once both are released.
- **Look at the board** (task 140, where it has landed): the stick and the
  look drag drive the free look over the frozen board, and board actions
  are ignored as task 140 rules.

### B. Actions
- **One state machine** (I-14, "What not to break"): every touch action
  drives the same `MouseActions` instance that the mouse buttons, the
  controller triggers and task 143's keys drive. A one-finger touch in the
  look zone is button 0, and a long-press or a second finger is button 2.
  No second release state machine is built.
- **Tap to reveal**: a touch in the right half presses button 0 when it
  lands. If it lifts before 400 ms having moved less than 8 px, button 0 is
  released and the crosshair cube is revealed on the lift (act on release).
  While the finger is down, the aimed cube shows what holding Left shows.
- **Drag to cancel**: once the touch moves 8 px or more it becomes a look
  drag. Its pending press is dropped without firing, and the lift acts on
  nothing. This is the same cancel as task 123's drag-to-look, where that
  has landed.
- **Long-press to flag**: a touch held 400 ms without moving 8 px drops its
  button 0 press without firing, then presses and releases button 2. That
  fires `right` on the crosshair cube while the finger is still down:
  flag or unflag, or on a number whatever a right click does there (task
  127's rules where they have landed). A short vibration
  (`navigator.vibrate`, where it exists) marks it, and the lift does
  nothing. The 400 ms and 8 px values are task 142's.
- **Two-finger tap to chord**: a second finger landing in the look zone
  while the first touch is still a pending tap presses button 2. Lifting
  either finger then fires `chord` on the crosshair cube through the
  state machine's chord toggle, in both lift orders, exactly as Left +
  Right do. If either finger moves 8 px, or the distance between them
  changes by 8 px, before the first lift, the gesture becomes a pinch
  (group C) and both presses are dropped without firing.
- **Accounting unchanged**: a touch action counts exactly as the mouse
  action it replaces in 3BV, clicks and efficiency, because it reaches the
  session through the same `onAction` path.
- **Gating**: touch actions act only where a mouse button acts on the
  board. That means 3D play, not paused, no failed-board offer and not
  during the end delay. Held touches are dropped without firing wherever
  held mouse buttons are: blur, tab hide, pause, a failed-board offer and
  leaving play. The browser's own touch gestures (page scroll, page zoom,
  double-tap zoom, the long-press context menu and text selection) do not
  fire on the scene during touch play, and emulated mouse events from a
  touch never reach the mouse listeners, so each gesture acts exactly once.

### C. Toolbar, pinch and pause
- **View-modes toolbar** (I-14, P-28): during touch play a toolbar of three
  toggle buttons sits above the scene, on the right edge clear of the
  overlay bar and the look zone's centre. The buttons are Shift
  (neighbours), Space (aim through numbers) and Ctrl (show hidden), each
  with a 44 px hit area, a visible pressed state and `aria-pressed`. A tap
  latches or clears its mode. The modes reach the renderer, picking and the
  3D sampler through `Controls` as one more source OR'd with the keyboard
  and the controller (as the controller's `pad` source is), so what each
  mode shows and how Space changes picking are unchanged. With no toolbar
  toggle on, `Controls` equals the keyboard and controller sources exactly.
  The toggles stay latched through pause and resume, and clear at a new
  game, restart and leaving play. A tap on the toolbar never reaches the
  scene gestures.
- **Pinch for spacing** (I-14): a two-finger pinch in the look zone changes
  the spacing through task 139's shared spacing function. Each named pinch
  increment of change in finger distance (a constant) is one step, and
  spreading the fingers means larger, as wheel up does. The range stays
  [1, 10], the camera follows the spacing by task 139's rule, and the
  spacing readout updates. A pinch fires no action.
- **Pause above the scene** (I-14): the overlay bar's `#hud-pause` stays
  above the scene and the touch-controls layer, with a 44 px hit area on
  coarse pointers. A tap pauses through `PAUSE.pause`. Resume from the
  pause card returns to lockless touch play.
- **Help**: `BINDINGS['3d']` in `js/settings/bindings.js` gains a touch
  device's rows: stick fly, right-half drag look, tap reveal, long-press
  flag, two-finger tap chord, pinch spacing, and the toolbar's three modes.
  The Settings page's Bindings panel, the controls modal and the in-game
  help (task 119's bindings component, where it has landed) list them from
  that one source. The coarse-only ready card lists the touch gestures in
  place of the key list. The menu's touch note (`NO_MOUSE_MSG`,
  `#touch-note`, as task 142 left it) says both modes play by touch.

### D. Orbit (task 146, where it has landed)
- **Same gestures, Orbit meanings** (task 146, I-15): with the Camera
  setting at Orbit, the right-half drag rotates around the board centre
  through Orbit's rotation path (0.2°/px, sensitivity, Invert Y, the pitch
  clamp). The stick's vertical axis dollies within Orbit's dolly range at
  Orbit's stick rate (up is in), and its horizontal axis does nothing. Tap,
  long-press, two-finger tap and the toolbar act exactly as in Fly. Pinch
  changes the spacing as in Fly, and Orbit's distance scales by task 146's
  spacing ratio rule.
- **Fly unchanged by Orbit's mapping**: with the Camera setting at Fly,
  every gesture behaves as groups A to C state.

### Cross-cutting
- **Mouse, keyboard and controller unchanged** ("What not to break":
  release-to-act with drag-to-cancel in both modes; the reference input
  conventions; 3BV, click and efficiency accounting). On a device that is
  not coarse-only, nothing in this task is mounted, and pointer lock, the
  ready card, mouse look, the wheel, the keys and the controller behave
  exactly as before. `tests/gamepad.test.mjs`, task 143's
  `tests/input-keyboard-3d.test.mjs` and task 146's
  `tests/camera-orbit.test.mjs` (where they exist) pass unedited.
  `FlyCamera` gives the same pose for the same mouse, key and stick
  inputs.
- **Replays unchanged**: a touch game records its actions by cell and its
  camera and view modes through the unchanged 3D sampler, with no change to
  the replay format, its version, `js/replay/sampler-3d.js` or
  `js/replay/viewer-3d.js`. The replays in `tests/fixtures/replays/` still
  verify.

## Decisions
- **Design change agreed (user decision, 2026-10-12):** settled by prior
  instruction in `docs/UX_REVIEW.md` (decision 11 and I-14, approved under
  § Approved scope and work plan, row W-23). The points below are stated
  against these documents as tasks 142, 143 and 146 leave them.
  - `product-design.md`, the "Mouse and keyboard first" point and the game
    shell's input sentence: touch is supported in 3D as well as in Classic
    2D, replacing "3D on a coarse-only device shows a notice until W-23".
  - `3d-play-flow.md` § Architecture, "Mode adapter": on a coarse-only
    device the ready card starts lockless touch play and pointer lock is
    never requested, replacing the notice with one Back action (P-39 →
    I-14). "3D game session": the actions come from the one mouse state
    machine fed by the mouse buttons, the controller triggers, the keyboard
    keys and touch.
  - `game-shell.md` § Architecture, "Pause controller": on a coarse-only
    device, resume returns to lockless touch play and never requests
    pointer lock.
  - `screen-components.md` § Architecture, "Overlay bar", and its
    composition list: during 3D touch play the overlay bar stays above the
    touch-controls layer, and the view-modes toolbar and the stick are
    compositions of the kit.
  - `settings.md` § Scope, the Controls item: look sensitivity and invert Y
    apply to touch look in 3D as well, and the bindings list covers touch.

  Settled here, without conflict: which devices run the touch controls
  (coarse-only), where a tap aims (the crosshair), and what pinch means in
  Orbit (spacing). See the decisions below. These documents are normally
  `/product-design`'s and `/architect`'s. This task updates them for
  exactly this and for nothing else. A further design decision met at
  implementation time is not covered: leave that passage untouched and name
  it under *Follow-ups* as a precise follow-up.
- **Touch controls run on coarse-only devices.** I-14's "coarse pointers"
  is read as task 142's `COARSE_ONLY` (coarse and not fine). Those are
  exactly the devices where task 142 shows the notice, so this task
  replaces the notice wherever it would appear. A hybrid device with a
  mouse keeps pointer-locked mouse play untouched, which is the "mouse play
  untouched" requirement.
- **A tap acts on the crosshair cube, not on the cube under the finger.**
  Picking from the screen centre is one of the reference input conventions
  "What not to break" protects. It is also how task 123's drag-to-look and
  task 146's Orbit aim. The player aims with the look drag and acts with a
  tap. Snap aiming (I-13) stays deferred.
- **Orbit: pinch changes the spacing; the stick dollies.** One gesture
  keeps one meaning in both cameras, and I-14 names pinch for spacing. In
  Orbit the wheel dollies, so task 146 moved spacing to `X` and the D-pad.
  A touch player has neither, so pinch is their only spacing control.
  Orbit's dolly comes from the stick's vertical axis, which mirrors task
  146's controller left stick, so Orbit loses nothing on touch.
- **No up and down controls.** Q and E have no touch counterpart. Task 139
  makes forward follow the view, so looking down and pushing the stick
  forward descends.
- **Touch feeds the shared state machine, like task 143's keys.** A
  one-finger touch maps to button 0, and a long-press or a second finger to
  button 2. Release-to-act, the chord toggle and the selection guard are
  therefore shared, and accounting is unchanged. The long-press acts at
  400 ms while the finger is held, as task 142's 2D long-press does, by
  dropping the pending button 0 press and then pressing and releasing
  button 2.
- **Toolbar toggles latch.** I-14 asks for toggles because a touch player
  cannot hold a key while flying and looking. They feed `Controls` as a
  source beside the keyboard and the controller, which keeps the renderer,
  picking and the sampler unedited. A latched view mode is a view choice,
  not a held action, so it survives pause. It clears with the game.
- **Thresholds are task 142's.** The 8 px move, 400 ms long-press and
  vibration match touch Classic 2D, so the two modes feel the same under
  the finger.
- **Out of scope**: touch Classic 2D and the touch-safe entry itself (W-22,
  task 142); keyboard-only 3D (W-24, task 143); Orbit itself (W-34, task
  146); camera presets and snap aiming (I-12, I-13, deferred); rebinding
  and the accessibility settings (P-44, P-56 to P-60, I-16, decision 17);
  touch in the replay viewer; touch controls on hybrid devices.
