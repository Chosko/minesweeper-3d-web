# Task 146 — Orbit camera mode

Target: claude

## Goal
Offer a second 3D camera, Orbit, beside today's first-person flight, chosen
by a Controls setting "Camera: Fly | Orbit" that defaults to Fly so nothing
changes for existing players. This is row W-34 of `docs/UX_REVIEW.md`
§ Approved scope and work plan (Area: 3D, input, Size: Medium, Phase 4,
after W-24). It closes I-15 of that document: first-person flight is the
biggest cognitive and vestibular cost of 3D (I-15, Medium effort). The
approved sketch is "Settings, Controls: Camera Fly or Orbit. Orbit keeps
the board centred; drag, stick or arrows rotate around it, wheel or
triggers dolly, the crosshair stays centred; keyboard look and keyboard
actions fall out of it". This task builds it with the triggers left as
reveal and flag (see Decisions). It serves motion-sensitive players,
keyboard-only players and newcomers who find flight disorienting. Orbit
reuses task 143's arrow-key look and its `Enter` / `'` actions. The
setting lives under Controls, not an Accessibility panel: decision 17 of
`docs/UX_REVIEW.md` drops that panel for now, so I-15's accessibility
framing is informational only.

## Acceptance criteria
- **The setting**: `js/settings/schema.js` gains `camera`, a choice of
  `fly` and `orbit` with default `fly`. It sits in the Settings page's
  Controls section after look sensitivity and invert Y, labelled "Camera",
  with the options "Fly" and "Orbit", as a segmented control generated
  from the schema. A saved settings document without the key loads as
  `fly` and keeps the settings version. An invalid value is refused like
  any other. Its applier, in `js/settings/appliers.js` and wired through
  `applySettings`, is the only writer of the 3D camera's mode. A change
  takes effect at once, also mid-game:
  - Fly to Orbit keeps the current yaw and pitch and takes the current
    distance to the board centre, clamped to the dolly range, then
    re-aims at the centre.
  - Orbit to Fly keeps the current pose unchanged as the fly pose.
- **Fly is unchanged**: with `camera` at `fly`, every 3D input behaves
  exactly as before this task. This covers mouse look, WASD / Q E flight,
  the wheel's spacing, the sticks, A / B, the triggers, the arrows,
  `Enter` / `'`, pointer lock and drag-to-look where it exists.
  `FlyCamera` gives the same pose for the same inputs.
- **The orbit camera**: in Orbit the pivot is the board's centre, the
  world origin where the grid is centred. The camera always looks at the
  pivot from a distance along its view line. The pose is held as yaw,
  pitch and distance, and it writes the same `x, y, z, yaw, pitch` fields
  `FlyCamera` exposes. Everything that reads the camera reads it unchanged:
  `apply`, picking from the screen centre, the render signature, the 3D
  replay sampler, the fly-through fade where task 139 has landed, and
  `Look at the board` where task 140 has landed. Rotation goes through the
  same path as fly look, at 0.2°/px. The sign is the same: mouse right
  turns the view right, so the camera swings around the board. The pitch
  clamp holds (±90°), Look sensitivity scales every rotation, and Invert Y
  flips its vertical part.
- **Rotating** in Orbit:
  - The mouse rotates under pointer lock exactly as it looks in Fly.
  - Task 123's lockless drag-to-look path, where it has landed, rotates by
    drag with the same threshold, so drag-to-cancel holds.
  - The right stick rotates at the controller look rate (180°/s).
  - The arrows rotate through task 143's ramped keyboard look (30°/s
    rising to 120°/s over 0.5 s).
  - `A` / `D` rotate left / right at the same rate as `ArrowLeft` /
    `ArrowRight`.
- **Dolly** in Orbit moves the camera along its view line, between a
  minimum of one lattice pitch (`4 × spacing`) and a maximum of three
  times the spawn distance (named constants in `js/input.js`):
  - The wheel dollies one notch at a time: wheel up moves in by a factor
    of 0.9 per notch, wheel down moves out by the inverse.
  - `W` / `S` dolly in and out, at 50 u/s while held.
  - The left stick's vertical axis dollies in proportion to its
    deflection, up to that rate (stick up = in). Its horizontal axis does
    nothing in Orbit.
  - A held dolly key or a deflected stick keeps the frame loop drawing, as
    flight does.
- **Unused in Orbit**: `Q` / `E` and the controller's `A` / `B` do nothing,
  so the board stays centred.
- **Spacing**: the wheel dollies in Orbit, so spacing stays on `X` (task
  139) and the controller's D-pad ↑ ↓. With the same step and readout as
  in Fly, a spacing change scales the orbit distance by the ratio of the
  new to the old spacing, mirroring task 139's camera-follows-spacing rule.
- **Picking**: the crosshair stays at the screen centre and picks through
  `pickCell` as today, so in Orbit it aims along the line from the camera
  through the board centre. A test pins that the cube nearest the camera
  on that line is the one picked, and that every surface cube of a 6 × 6 × 6
  box is picked from some orbit direction. Space's aim-through and the
  exclusions (unlinked cells; pressed cells while Space is held) are
  unchanged.
- **Actions**: reveal, flag and chord work in Orbit exactly as in Fly, from
  every device on the same `MouseActions` instance, with release-to-act,
  the chord toggle and drag-to-cancel unchanged:
  - mouse Left / Right;
  - controller RT / LT, with LT + RT to chord;
  - keyboard `Enter` / `'`, with both together to chord.

  The Shift / Space / Ctrl view modes and LB / RB / Y work exactly as in
  Fly.
- **Start and Back to start**: a new 3D game in Orbit starts at the orbit
  pose derived from the spawn pose (task 125's where it has landed, else
  today's start pose): its yaw and pitch, its distance to the board
  centre, re-aimed at the centre. `Home` / `R` and the pause card's "Back
  to start" (task 125) put the orbit back to that pose, at the current
  spacing, with nothing else changed.
- **Look at the board** (task 140, where it has landed): the Camera
  setting drives the free look over the frozen finished board. A player
  who chose Orbit orbits it, starting from the pose the game ended with.
  The camera and inputs are those of Orbit play, with board actions
  ignored as task 140 rules.
- **Pointer lock**: Orbit starts and pauses exactly as Fly does. The ready
  card's click or `Enter` takes pointer lock, losing lock pauses, and the
  lockless and controller paths are the same.
- **Replays**: an Orbit game records its camera through the unchanged 3D
  sampler as ordinary position, yaw and pitch samples, with no change to
  the replay format, its version, `js/replay/sampler-3d.js` or
  `js/replay/viewer-3d.js`. `tests/replay-3d.test.mjs` records a short
  Orbit game (rotate, dolly, reveal, flag) and checks it replays to the
  same final state with the recorded camera. The replays in
  `tests/fixtures/replays/` still verify.
- **Bindings and help**: the bindings source in `js/settings/bindings.js`
  carries the 3D rows for the chosen camera. In Orbit these are:
  - keyboard and mouse: mouse / arrows / `A` `D` rotate around the board,
    the wheel / `W` `S` dolly, `Q` `E` unused;
  - controller: right stick rotates, left stick up / down dollies.

  The rows are kept in one table, selected by the camera, so no hand-written
  copy exists. The Settings page's Bindings panel, the controls modal and
  the in-game help (task 119's bindings component where it has landed)
  show the chosen camera's rows and follow a change of the setting. While
  the ready card's hand-written key list (`index.html` `.ready-keys`)
  exists, it shows the chosen camera's movement line.
- **Tests first**: a new `tests/camera-orbit.test.mjs` pins:
  - the orbit pose: the line of sight through the board centre for any
    yaw, pitch and distance;
  - rotation: the 0.2°/px rate, the sign, sensitivity, Invert Y and the
    pitch clamp;
  - the dolly: its range clamps, the wheel factor, the `W` / `S` and stick
    rate;
  - the spacing ratio, the start pose and Back to start;
  - both switches between Fly and Orbit;
  - the picking criteria above;
  - `FlyCamera` poses unchanged for the same inputs.

  `tests/settings-store.test.mjs`, `tests/settings-appliers.test.mjs` and
  `tests/settings-page.test.mjs` pin the schema entry, its default and
  refusal, the missing-key load, the applier and the Controls section and
  Orbit binding rows. `tests/gamepad.test.mjs` passes unedited. A
  Playwright check plays a Cube Beginner board in Orbit, checked by eye:
  - with the mouse: rotate, wheel dolly, reveal, flag, chord;
  - with the keyboard alone: arrows, `W` / `S`, `Enter`, `'`;
  - with a faked controller: right stick, left stick, triggers;
  - Back to start;
  - then confirms Fly plays as before.
- **What not to break** (`docs/UX_REVIEW.md` § Decisions taken):
  - release-to-act with drag-to-cancel in both modes;
  - the reference input conventions: in Fly, 0.2°/px look, the pitch
    clamp, 50 u/s and the forward vector as task 139 leaves it; in both
    cameras, picking from the screen centre and the Shift / Space / Ctrl
    view modes;
  - 3BV, click and efficiency accounting;
  - replays recorded by earlier builds.
- **Documentation**:
  - `README.md`'s § 3D controls gains the Camera setting and the Orbit
    controls.
  - `docs/ORIGINAL_SPEC.md` keeps describing the original. It gains a
    "Web port:" note, in the form of the existing ones, under § Controls
    on the free-fly camera line: an optional Orbit camera, default Fly, Fly
    unchanged.
- `.claude/domain/product-design.md` states the agreed design change in
  every passage that stated the old one, starting with § 3D mode's
  "flown through in first person". The change is that the 3D box is flown
  through in first person or, by a Controls setting, orbited around its
  centre. Nothing else in the document changes.
- `.claude/domain/features/settings.md` states the agreed design change in
  every passage that stated the old one (§ Scope's Controls item, the
  saved settings list, the settings page's Controls section). The change
  is that Controls gains "Camera: Fly | Orbit", default Fly, stored in the
  settings document. Nothing else in the document changes.
- `.claude/domain/features/3d-results-records-screens.md`, where task 140
  has written its Look at the board passage, settles which camera drives
  that free look: the one the Camera setting chooses. Nothing else in the
  document changes.

## Decisions
- **Design change agreed (user decision, 2026-10-12):** settled by prior
  instruction in `docs/UX_REVIEW.md` (I-15, approved under § Approved scope
  and work plan, row W-34).
  - `product-design.md` § 3D mode says the box is "flown through in first
    person". By the Camera setting it can also be orbited (I-15).
  - `features/settings.md` § Scope lists Controls as look sensitivity,
    invert Y and the bindings list. It gains "Camera: Fly | Orbit",
    default Fly, saved with the other settings (I-15: "Settings, Controls:
    Camera Fly or Orbit"; decision 17 keeps it out of an Accessibility
    panel).

  Settled here, without conflict:
  - The Camera setting drives task 140's Look at the board.
    `3d-results-records-screens.md` and decision 4 ("Yes, free flight over
    the frozen board") settle that the player gets a free look, not which
    camera drives it (decision 4, I-15).
  - `3d-play-flow.md` § Architecture's action sources (mouse buttons,
    controller triggers, task 143's keys on one state machine) stay as
    they are.

  These documents are normally `/product-design`'s and `/architect`'s; this
  task updates them for exactly this and for nothing else. A further
  design decision met at implementation time is not covered: leave that
  passage untouched and name it under *Follow-ups* as a precise follow-up.
- **Correction of I-15's sketch: the triggers do not dolly** (I-15, W-34;
  user decision 2026-10-12). The sketch's "wheel or triggers dolly" missed
  that RT / LT already reveal and flag on the shared release state machine.
  A trigger cannot both dolly while held and act on release, and the
  requirement of one state machine with the same actions on every device
  wins. The sketch reads as "wheel or left stick dolly". The left stick is
  free in Orbit because there is no flight.
- **The crosshair aims along the camera-to-centre line.** The orbit camera
  always looks at the pivot, and picking stays at the screen centre as
  I-15 asks. Every cube is reached by orbiting to its direction from the
  centre, with Space's aim-through for revealed numbers in the way. Camera
  presets and snap aiming (I-12, I-13) stay deferred.
- **`Q` / `E` and the controller's `A` / `B` are unused in Orbit.** Moving
  the pivot would break "Orbit keeps the board centred".
- **`W` / `S` dolly and `A` / `D` rotate** in Orbit, so a keyboard-only
  player has a dolly without a wheel, and WASD keeps a meaning on an orbit
  camera.
- **Pointer lock works as in Fly**, so the start, pause-on-unlock and
  controller flows are one flow. Task 123's lockless drag-to-look path
  orbits by drag, which is I-15's "drag".
- **Spacing stays on `X` and the D-pad** in Orbit, since the wheel dollies
  there. The mouse loses the wheel's fine spacing only in the camera the
  player chose.
- **The orbit camera exposes `FlyCamera`'s pose fields.** That keeps the
  sampler, viewer, picking and renderer unedited and the replay format
  unchanged. A switch between cameras keeps the view where it is, rather
  than jumping to a spawn pose.
- **Out of scope:**
  - a pause-card shortcut for the Camera setting (the Settings page holds
    it);
  - rebinding and presets (P-44, waits for `input-rebinding`);
  - camera presets and snap aiming (I-12, I-13, deferred);
  - an Accessibility panel, Reduce motion and the other accessibility
    settings (P-56 to P-60, I-16, decision 17);
  - touch controls for 3D (W-23);
  - the replay viewer's free camera (I-10, W-32);
  - Classic 2D.
