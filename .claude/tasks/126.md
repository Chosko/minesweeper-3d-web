# Task 126 — 3D small feedback

Target: claude

## Goal
Three small pieces of 3D feedback: let Ctrl show hidden cells without
walling off the board, mark a number's auto-hide as a reward, and make a
quick double Esc after the pointer-lock release do what the player meant.
This is row W-19 of `docs/UX_REVIEW.md` § Approved scope and work plan
(Area: 3D, Size: Small, Phase 1). It closes P-29, P-35 and P-36 of that
document, all approved without condition. Today holding Ctrl (show hidden)
draws every hidden cell as an opaque cube with its blank or number tile, so
from the front it is a solid white wall covering every number (P-29, Minor,
every 3D player). Auto-hide happens silently: cubes vanish after a flag and
the newcomer cannot tell a reward from a mistake (P-35, Minor, newcomer to
3D). And a second Esc within 500 ms of the pointer-lock release is ignored,
so a quick double Esc appears to do nothing and the player must click Resume
(P-36, Minor, 3D mouse players). The work serves every 3D player, the
newcomer to 3D most.

## Acceptance criteria
- **Ctrl ghosts** (P-29): while Ctrl (or its controller binding) is held,
  every hidden cell — a revealed zero or a hidden (unlinked) number — is
  drawn with its tile at alpha 0.25 in the transparent pass, sorted
  back-to-front with the other transparent cubes, instead of opaque. Cubes
  behind a ghost stay visible through it. The ghost alpha is one named
  constant. Releasing Ctrl hides them again as today. Hidden cells stay
  unpickable under Ctrl (picking is unchanged). The vertex shader's alpha
  table, `_writeCell`, `_rebuildOpaque`, `_applyShiftOpaque` and
  `_updateTransparent` stay in step: with Shift and Space held together
  with Ctrl the ghosts keep alpha 0.25 and Shift's and Space's own fades are
  unchanged. A loss wave still shows and shrinks hidden cells as today,
  with or without Ctrl.
- **"Solved" pulse** (P-35): when an action makes a number (1 or more) that
  was visible become hidden, the cube is not removed at once: it plays a
  short "solved" pulse (a brief brightening and scale-up of the cube, about
  0.2 to 0.3 s), then fades out (about 0.2 s), then is gone. Durations and
  peak values are named constants; the whole sequence lasts under half a
  second. Revealed zeros, which are never visible, do not pulse. Many cubes
  hidden by one action pulse together. The pulse is visual only: the
  engine hides the cell in the same action as today, so picking passes
  through it at once, and no game state, action, replay format or stat
  changes. A cell shown again (a neighbour unflagged) during its pulse
  drops the pulse and draws normally; a loss during a pulse drops it and
  the loss wave proceeds as today; Ctrl held during a pulse lets the pulse
  finish into a ghost. `isAnimating()` is true while any pulse runs and
  false once the last ends, so render-on-demand draws the pulse and then
  stops drawing. The per-frame cost is bounded by the number of pulsing
  cells, never O(n) in the board.
- **Pulse in replays** (P-35): the 3D replay viewer shows the same pulse
  when its playback hides a number, since it draws through the same
  renderer; a seek drops every running pulse at once (as `_endEffects`
  drops the end-of-game effects), so a seek never leaves a pulsing cube.
- **Double Esc** (P-36), one of two branches, chosen by investigation:
  - **Branch 1, preferred** (`docs/UX_REVIEW.md` P-36, first option):
    ignore only the Esc press that released the pointer lock. Any later Esc
    press on the pause card of a game still in play resumes exactly as a
    keyboard Back does today (`PAUSE.resume('key')`), however soon after
    the release. The 500 ms time window in `resumeFromPause` goes.
  - **Branch 2, fallback** (P-36, second option): taken only when the
    implementer finds that the releasing Esc cannot be told apart from a
    deliberate second Esc in the browsers the game supports (Chromium and
    Firefox at least) — for example, when whether its `keydown` or `keyup`
    reaches the page varies by browser in a way no rule covers. The 500 ms
    window stays, and the pause card of a 3D game still in play shows the
    line "Press Esc or click Resume" among its status lines. The line is
    hidden in Classic 2D and on an ended 3D game.
  The branch taken and the evidence for it (which events each browser
  delivers for the releasing Esc) are written under the task's
  implementation notes or commit body and in `.claude/context/app-shell.md`
  § the pause flow. Either way: Esc still pauses from pointer-locked play
  (the browser's lock release); a controller or a pause button still
  pauses and resumes as today; held Esc auto-repeat never resumes.
- **Tests first**: a unit test pins the pulse envelope (a pure function of
  elapsed time giving the cube's scale, brightness and alpha: rising then
  fading, alpha 0 and done at the end) and the ghost alpha constant. A
  browser test through `__ms` holds Ctrl on a board with hidden cells and
  checks that they count in `stats.trans`, not `stats.opaque`; that a flag
  which hides a number makes `isAnimating()` true and then false within the
  pulse's length; that unflagging during the pulse ends it. The Esc rule is
  pinned in a unit test as a pure decision (under branch 1: the releasing
  press is ignored and the next press resumes at any delay; under branch 2:
  the window holds and the pause card shows the line for a 3D game in play
  only). A Playwright check screenshots Ctrl held on Cube Intermediate
  mid-game and the pulse mid-way, checked by eye.
- **What not to break** (`docs/UX_REVIEW.md` § Decisions taken):
  release-to-act with drag-to-cancel; the reference input conventions,
  including the Shift / Space / Ctrl view modes and picking from the screen
  centre; 3BV, click and efficiency accounting; the confirmations that say
  "It will count as a loss"; replays recorded by earlier builds and the
  recorded games under `tests/fixtures/replays/`, which still verify.
- `docs/ORIGINAL_SPEC.md` keeps the original's drawing rules (Ctrl draws
  hidden cells opaque; unlinked cells are invisible) and gains "Web port:"
  notes, like the one on `Esc` / `F5`, saying that the port draws hidden
  cells under Ctrl as 25 % ghosts and plays a short pulse before a number
  hides. README.md's Ctrl row and its "Solved cells hide themselves" line
  match, and § Gameplay fidelity lists both as deliberate changes.
- `.claude/domain/product-design.md` states the agreed design change — the
  3D mode keeps its look except that Ctrl shows hidden cells as translucent
  ghosts and a number pulses before it hides — in every passage that stated
  the old one. Nothing else in the document changes.

## Decisions
- **Design change agreed (user decision, 2026-10-12):** settled by prior
  instruction — `docs/UX_REVIEW.md` P-29 and P-35, approved without
  condition under § Approved scope and work plan (row W-19).
  - `product-design.md` § Design decisions, "The 3D mode is the existing 3D
    game", says its look is kept; Ctrl's drawing of hidden cells changes
    from opaque to 25 % ghosts (P-29), and a number plays a short pulse
    before it hides (P-35).

  Settled here, without conflict: the 500 ms Esc window (P-36) is not
  stated in any owned document; the pulse and the ghosts keep the 3D scene
  free of theme tokens, as `product-design.md` § Visual design system
  requires. `product-design.md` is normally `/product-design`'s; this task
  updates it for exactly this and for nothing else. A further design
  decision met at implementation time is not covered: leave that passage
  untouched and name it under *Follow-ups* as a precise follow-up.
- **The original spec's drawing rules change on purpose** (P-29, P-35).
  `docs/ORIGINAL_SPEC.md` § Controls and § Drawing rules say Ctrl draws
  hidden cells opaque with their number tile and that unlinked cells are
  invisible. The review approved both changes outright, with no setting to
  keep the original look; the spec keeps describing the original and gains
  "Web port:" notes.
- **The pulse is visual only.** "Before auto-hide fades it" is read as a
  renderer effect after the engine has hidden the cell, not a delay in the
  engine: the rules, picking, the 3D state view, replays and their
  determinism stay exactly as they are, and the original's "unlinked cells
  are unpickable" holds from the action on.
- **Only numbers pulse.** P-35's problem is a cube the player could see
  vanishing; revealed zeros are never drawn, so they have nothing to pulse.
- **The P-36 branch is the implementer's call, by evidence.** The review
  gives the order of preference; which branch holds depends on what the
  browsers deliver for the Esc that releases pointer lock, which is only
  known by testing. Branch 2 is not taken for convenience.
- **Out of scope:** the help line "a number disappears once it is solved"
  from P-35's proposed solution belongs to task 119 (row W-01), whose 3D
  rules block ("empty and solved cubes hide themselves, and holding Ctrl
  shows them") and one-time auto-hide toast carry it; this task adds no
  help copy. Everything else in `docs/UX_REVIEW.md`, including W-18's
  readability work (P-23) and W-15's fly-through fade (P-25).
- **Boundary with task 119** (row W-01): 119 redraws the pause card's
  controls list and help. Under branch 2 the "Press Esc or click Resume"
  line is a pause status line beside `#pause-sub`, not part of the controls
  list, so either task can land first.
- **Boundary with task 125** (row W-14): 125 also edits the 3D mode bullet
  of `product-design.md` and adds a ground grid in `js/render.js`;
  whichever task lands second keeps the other's change and adds its own.
