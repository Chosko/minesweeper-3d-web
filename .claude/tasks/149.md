# Task 149 — Richer Records and local top-10

Target: claude

## Goal
Give the Records screen the figures a grinder reads to follow their own
progression on a board: a personal top-10, averages, a recent win rate, the
best time's progression by date, filters and an export. This is row W-31 of
`docs/UX_REVIEW.md` § Approved scope and work plan (Area: Records, Size:
Medium, Phase 5, after W-30). It closes I-05 (Local top-10 per board,
Small, grinders) and I-09 (Richer Records, Small to medium, every
enthusiast) of that document. Every leaderboard feature in the design
depends on Steam, yet the data for a personal top-N is already in the
history (I-05); Records shows bests and a chart, but grinders read averages
and progression (I-09). The approved solutions: a Top 10 tab per board,
ranked by time, 3BV/s and efficiency, each row with its date, 3BV, clicks
and Watch, with the results screen's "#4 of 57" (I-05); averages over the
last 10, 50 and all games for time, 3BV/s and efficiency, a win rate over
the last 50, a best-time-by-date chart beside the 3BV/s chart with a dated
axis and hover values, filters by period and by outcome, and a CSV or JSON
export (I-09). The results screen's rank that task 128 adds stays the
single rank computation. The work extends task 148's Records layout (stat
tiles, pager, column layout), and its export takes the place task 131 left
for it in the Settings page's Data panel. Both sides of the Records 2D | 3D
switch get every change.

## Acceptance criteria
- **One ranking** (I-05): a pure, DOM-free function in `js/records/` (for
  example `rankedGames(history, stat)` in a new `js/records/stats.js`)
  orders a board's won games by one of `BEST_STATS` — lower time, higher
  3BV/s, higher efficiency — with a tie going to the earlier game, the
  records' tie rule; a game whose stat is null (`isBestEligible` false) is
  left out of that stat's ranking. The results flow's "#n of N on this
  board" (task 128) reads the finished game's position in the by-time
  ranking from this function, replacing its own computation, so the
  results line and the Top 10 by time can never disagree. N counts the
  board's won games, this one included, as task 128 defines it; the
  records model, its documents and its comparison are unchanged. Rank 1 by
  each stat is the game holding that best in the bests tiles.
- **Top 10 tab** (I-05): the Records screen's games area gains two tabs,
  "Recent games" (task 148's paged list, the default) and "Top 10". The Top
  10 shows the chosen board's ten best won games under a Time · 3BV/s ·
  Efficiency choice (Time by default), each row with its rank, time,
  3BV/s, efficiency, date and time (task 148's date-and-time format), the
  board's 3BV, the counted clicks (`countedClicks`), and Watch when the
  game's replay is kept and the replay route is registered, in the results
  formats. Fewer than ten won games shows them all; none shows an honest
  empty line ("No won games on this board yet."). A no-guess board ranks
  only its own games. The tabs and the stat choice use the kit (the
  segmented choice, or a tab component added to the kit and catalogued in
  `css/components.css`, shown in `dev/components.html` and listed in
  `tests/components.test.mjs`); every control is operable by mouse,
  keyboard and controller, and when task 144 has landed they are stops on
  its Records walk in DOM order.
- **Averages and recent win rate** (I-09): under the board's counters, an
  averages panel shows the mean time, 3BV/s and efficiency over the
  board's last 10, last 50 and all won games, as a small table (rows Time,
  3BV/s, Efficiency; columns Last 10, Last 50, All) in the results formats,
  and the win rate over the board's last 50 games of every outcome. With
  fewer games than a window holds, that column averages what there is and
  says how many ("Last 50 (12)"); no won game shows dashes. Once task 130
  has landed, the completed-games win rate over the same last 50 games
  sits beside the reference one (`docs/UX_REVIEW.md` § Decisions taken,
  decision 8). The averages are derived from the history on every draw and
  stored nowhere.
- **Best-time-by-date chart** (I-09): beside the 3BV/s and efficiency
  chart (side by side in task 148's wide layout, stacked below 1024 px), a
  second canvas chart shows the board's best time by date: a step line of
  the standing best time, stepping down at each new best, with a marker at
  each new best, against a dated horizontal axis (the first and last date
  of the shown games and evenly spaced date ticks in between, in a short
  date form such as "9 Oct") and a time axis from 0. It is drawn on a
  canvas from the design tokens in both themes, redrawn on theme change and
  resize like the existing chart, with no chart library and no literal
  colour. It is `role="img"` with a text alternative naming each new best
  and its date, and an honest empty line without a won game.
- **Hover values** (I-09): on both charts, a pointer hovering the plot
  shows the nearest point's values in a small readout — on the best-time
  chart its date and best time, on the 3BV/s chart that game's date, 3BV/s
  and efficiency — with a marker on the point; it disappears when the
  pointer leaves. A tap on a touch screen shows the same readout. Keyboard
  and controller players read the same numbers in the text alternatives
  and the lists, so the charts take no focus stop.
- **Filters by period and outcome** (I-09): above the history area, a
  period choice (All time · 12 months · 30 days · 7 days, measured back
  from now on each game's end date) and an outcome choice (All · Won ·
  Lost · Abandoned). The period filters every history view of the chosen
  board: the recent-games list, the Top 10, the averages, the recent win
  rate and both charts. The outcome filters the recent-games list; the
  won-only views (Top 10, averages, charts) say they count won games. The
  bests tiles, the board's counters and the mode's overall row stay
  all-time and unfiltered. Changing a filter starts the recent-games list
  on its first page; the filters are screen state, kept across board and
  mode changes while the screen is open and back to All time · All on each
  `show`. A filter that leaves nothing shows the views' empty lines, never
  the board's empty state. Live update redraws through the filters.
- **Export** (I-09, P-70): one export module (for example
  `js/records/export.js`) builds two files from the records store's
  history of every board in both modes and the replay library's listing of
  every kept replay:
  - **JSON** — `minesweeper-3d-records-<YYYY-MM-DD>.json`, one object
    `{ format: 1, exportedAt, games: [...], replays: [...] }`: each game
    its history entry plus its board label and its derived 3BV/s and
    efficiency (unrounded); each replay its listing fields and pins.
  - **CSV** — `minesweeper-3d-history-<YYYY-MM-DD>.csv`, one row per game,
    newest first, with a header row: ended at (ISO 8601), mode, board key,
    board label, no-guess, outcome, time in seconds (three decimals), 3BV,
    3BV solved, 3BV/s (three decimals), efficiency (one decimal), counted
    clicks, then reveal, flag and chord effective and wasted, replay kept
    (yes/no) and replay pins. Fields are quoted and escaped per RFC 4180,
    with a dot decimal and no units.
  The replay files themselves are not exported (W-32). The download goes
  through a Blob and an object URL with an injected trigger, so tests
  observe the file without downloading. Exporting writes no document. With
  no game recorded, the export actions are disabled with a line saying
  there is nothing to export yet. The replay library gains a query of
  every entry across boards (for example `entries()`, newest first, frozen
  like `forBoard`); a library that failed to load exports an empty replay
  list.
- **Export in two places** (I-09, P-70): the Settings page's Data panel
  (task 131) shows "Export history (CSV)" and "Export all (JSON)" before
  "Reset all data…", so an export is offered first, with a line saying
  what they save; the Records screen shows the same two actions below its
  lists, beside Back. Both call the one export module, so both give the
  same files. The reset confirmation's wording is unchanged.
- **Navigation and layout hold**: the default focus stays the chosen board
  in the picker; Tab order follows the visual order in task 148's one- and
  two-column layouts; at 390 px nothing scrolls horizontally; LB / RB / LT
  / RT keep switching boards and modes when task 144 has landed.
- **What not to break** (`docs/UX_REVIEW.md` § What not to break): 3BV,
  click and efficiency accounting (every figure goes through
  `js/records/summary.js`'s derivations); no-guess boards as separate
  records; the three-way best comparison; auto-pinned best replays; the
  honest empty states and storage notices — the records not-saved note and
  the replays-not-saved notice, each told once per session as today.
- Tests come first: the ranking for each stat (order, the tie to the
  earlier game, null stats left out) and the results flow's rank read from
  it (agreeing with the Top 10 by time); the Top 10 content (ten rows,
  fewer, none, Watch only for kept replays, no-guess separation); the
  averages (each window, short windows with their count, no won game) and
  the last-50 win rate; the best-time step points, the dated axis ticks,
  the hover lookup of the nearest point on both charts and the text
  alternative; the period and outcome filters on each view and their reset
  on `show`; the CSV (header, escaping, rounding, order) and the JSON
  shape, the disabled empty export and the injected download; the
  library's all-entries query; the Settings Data panel's export actions
  before Reset and the Records export actions. The full suite passes. A
  Playwright check through `__ms` at 1280 × 800 and 390 × 844, in both
  themes, opens the Top 10, hovers both charts, changes the filters and
  exports both files.
- `README.md`'s Records section mentions the Top 10, the averages, the
  best-time chart, the filters and the export, and its Settings line the
  export in the Data panel.
- The owned documents state the agreed design change — period and outcome
  filters and a CSV and JSON export of the history and the replays'
  listing; a Top 10 tab, averages over the last 10, 50 and all won games, a
  last-50 win rate and a best-time-by-date chart in the stats history; the
  replay files themselves still not exported — in every passage that
  stated the old one, and settle the hover values on both charts, the
  library's all-entries query and the export's place in the Settings Data
  panel. The documents are `.claude/domain/features/records-screen.md`,
  `.claude/domain/features/replay-library.md` and
  `.claude/domain/features/settings.md`. Nothing else in those documents
  changes.

## Decisions
- **Design change agreed (user decision, 2026-10-12):** settled by prior
  instruction — `docs/UX_REVIEW.md` I-05 and I-09, approved without
  condition under § Approved scope and work plan (row W-31).
  - `records-screen.md` § Scope and non-goals lists "Filtering history by
    date range or exporting it" as a non-goal; the screen gains period and
    outcome filters and an export (I-09).
  - `records-screen.md` § Scope and non-goals gives the stats history as
    the 3BV/s and efficiency chart and the recent-games list; it gains the
    Top 10 tab (I-05), the averages, the last-50 win rate and the
    best-time-by-date chart (I-09), and § Architecture describes them.
  - `replay-library.md` § Scope and non-goals lists "Importing, exporting
    or sharing replays" as a non-goal; the Records export carries the
    library's listing of kept replays (I-09, row W-31); importing,
    exporting or sharing the replay files stays out of this task (W-32,
    I-10).

  Settled here, without conflict: hover values on both charts
  (`records-screen.md` § Architecture, "History chart"); the library's
  all-entries query (`replay-library.md` § Interfaces and contracts); the
  export actions in the Settings Data panel before "Reset all data…"
  (`settings.md` § Architecture, Settings page; P-70 "with an export
  offered first"). These documents are normally `/architect`'s; this task
  updates them for exactly this and for nothing else. A further design
  decision met at implementation time is not covered: leave that passage
  untouched and name it under *Follow-ups* as a precise follow-up.
- **One ranking function, in records.** The review requires the results
  screen's "#4 of 57" to stay the single rank computation; moving task
  128's rule into a pure records function that both the results flow and
  the Top 10 call is what makes that hold, while the records model and its
  documents stay unchanged.
- **Averages count won games.** A lost or abandoned game's time is not a
  finishing time, and every best already counts won games only; the last
  10, 50 and all are the board's last won games. The win rate over the
  last 50 counts every outcome, like the reference win rate.
- **Dated axis on the best-time chart only.** The 3BV/s chart keeps its
  per-game axis, since a dated axis would stack a day's games into one
  column; I-09 asks the dated axis of the new chart, and hover values go on
  both so the two read alike.
- **Which views the filters reach.** The period applies to every history
  view; the outcome applies to the recent-games list, the only view that
  shows losses. The bests, counters and overall figures stay all-time,
  since they are the records themselves. The filters are screen state and
  not remembered; P-53's remembered Pinned first (task 148) is the screen's
  one persisted preference.
- **Both formats, split by use.** The CSV is the history as one table for
  a spreadsheet, with the replay state as columns, since every kept
  replay's id is its game's id; the JSON carries the history and the full
  replay listing for a complete copy. One module gives both, from Settings
  and from Records, and exports everything rather than the filtered view,
  so the copy offered before a reset is whole.
- **Out of scope:** replay file download and import belong to W-32 (I-10);
  the daily board's separate Records board belongs to W-33 (I-03); records
  durability (P-49, P-50) is dropped by decision 9; the accessibility
  settings are not now (decision 17).
