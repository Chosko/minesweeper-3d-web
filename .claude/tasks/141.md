# Task 141 — 3D readability

Target: claude+human

## Goal
Make it readable which 26 cells a 3D number counts, and how many of them
are still closed or flagged. This task is row W-18 of `docs/UX_REVIEW.md`
§ Approved scope and work plan (Area: 3D, Size: Iterative, Phase 3,
attended). It closes P-23 of that document, under the condition in
§ Approved scope and work plan: "P-23 is built in attended iterations with
feedback before each is accepted." Today the player has to imagine which
26 cells a number means. A 1 in the front layer and a 1 two layers back look
identical: perspective is weak, the fog never reaches Beginner and there is
no layer shading. Numbers at different depths stack in one screen column.
Shift mode's near 3 × 3 layer hides the middle and far ones. Hidden zeros
leave gaps the player has to remember, and nothing says how many of the 26
are closed or flagged. 3D deduction is counting, and today the player
counts by flying around and tallying three layers by eye (P-23, Major). The
work serves every 3D player from the second click on, and the Enthusiast
and Cube Expert most (`docs/UX_REVIEW.md` § personas). It decides whether 3D
is deduced or brute-forced. The approved solution is built as five
iterations, in the order below, and the owner reviews each one before the
next starts (§ Manual interventions).

## Acceptance criteria
The iterations are built in this order, one at a time. Each one ends at its
checkpoint in § Manual interventions. An iteration the owner approves keeps
its criteria below as written, or as the owner adjusted them. An iteration
the owner drops leaves no code, test or doc note behind, and its criteria
no longer apply. The criteria after the iterations hold for whatever ships.

**Iteration 1: aimed-cell readout** (P-23 solution 1)
- During 3D play, while the crosshair is on a revealed number (1 or more),
  a readout beside the crosshair shows that number and its neighbourhood in
  the review's form: `2 · 5 closed · 1 flag` (singular and plural: `1 flag`
  and `2 flags`). Here *closed* means a neighbour that is neither revealed
  nor flagged, and *flag* means a flagged neighbour. Hidden (unlinked)
  neighbours are revealed, so they count as neither. A cell on the box's
  edge or corner counts only the neighbours inside the box.
- The readout says when an action is certain. When the flags equal the
  number and at least one closed neighbour is left, it says a chord applies.
  When the closed neighbours equal the number minus the flags, and that is
  at least 1, it says flagging them is certain (the flag-all on a number,
  which task 127 keeps). Otherwise it says neither, including when the flags
  exceed the number. The exact cue words are named constants, and the owner
  settles them at checkpoint 1.
- One pure, DOM-free function computes the readout from the 3D state view
  and a cell index: number, closed, flags, chord applies, certain flag. It
  reads `X`, `Y`, `Z`, `idx`/`coords`, `number`, `pressed` and `flagged`
  (`.claude/context/engine.md` § 3D state view) and walks the 26 lattice
  neighbours. It adds nothing to the engine or the state view.
- The readout is hidden in these cases: the crosshair is on no cell, a
  closed cell, a flag or a zero; before the first reveal; while paused;
  during the end delay and after the game ends (a loss shows every cell
  pressed); in Classic 2D; on the menu backdrop; and in the 3D replay
  viewer. It updates whenever the aimed cell changes or the board's
  `version` moves, on the pick path that already runs (`updatePick`), so it
  adds no frames while nothing moves. It writes to the DOM only when its
  text changes.
- It sits beside the crosshair without covering the aimed cube. It is
  readable over the 3D scene in both themes, styled from tokens with no
  literal colour (`.claude/context/app-shell.md`), and it works in
  pointer-locked and lockless controller play alike. The debug hook's
  `info()` reports its text, and the `__ms` key list stays unchanged.

**Iteration 2: neighbourhood wireframe** (P-23 solution 2)
- While the crosshair is on a revealed number, a thin wireframe outlines
  that number's 3 × 3 × 3 block at all times, whatever Shift, Space and
  Ctrl do. The block is clipped to the box, so an edge or corner cell's
  outline encloses only the cells that exist. The outline follows the
  spacing and is hidden when the aim leaves a number, and in the same cases
  as the readout, except that it also draws in the 3D replay viewer
  (below).
- The wireframe is one small mesh (line segments) placed by uniforms or a
  transform, so it adds no per-frame O(n) work. It is never pickable, never
  hides a number behind it, and needs no theme token: the renderer stays
  unthemed (`.claude/context/rendering.md` § Not themed). A pure function
  gives the block's clipped lattice bounds for a cell.

**Iteration 3: Shift shows the whole block** (P-23 solution 3)
- With Shift held and a number aimed, the block's three layers are told
  apart by a tint, a separation, or both, as the owner prefers at
  checkpoint 3: near, middle and far. The layers are cut along the box
  axis closest to the view direction, with the near layer on the camera's
  side. One pure function gives each block cell's layer (0, 1 or 2) from
  the camera's position and view direction. The near layer no longer hides
  the middle and far ones: a closed or flagged block cell behind another
  stays visible.
- Every in-box position of the block draws something under Shift. A
  hidden (unlinked) cell there, whether a revealed zero or an auto-hidden
  number, draws as a ghost outline: edges only, no tile, never pickable.
  So a block with all 26 neighbours in the box always shows 26 positions
  around the aimed cube. With Ctrl also held, task 126's Ctrl ghost (its
  tile at alpha 0.25) takes the place of the outline when that task has
  landed.
- Shift's other rules stay as they are: cells outside the block fade to
  alpha 0.2, and the aimed number stays opaque. The vertex shader's alpha
  table, `_writeCell`, `_rebuildOpaque`, `_applyShiftOpaque` and
  `_updateTransparent` stay in step, and transparent cubes still draw back
  to front.

**Iteration 4: depth dimming under Space** (P-23 solution 4)
- With Space held, a revealed number's alpha falls with its lattice
  distance from the camera, so of two numbers stacked in one screen column
  the nearer one draws brighter and is the one read. The curve is one pure
  function with named constants (the nearest numbers' alpha and the floor)
  that are also interpolated into the shader, so the shader and its test
  share one source. The curve does not increase with distance. Space's pick
  rule does not change: the crosshair still passes through revealed cells.

**Iteration 5: depth shading by lattice distance** (P-23 solution 5)
- In every view mode, a cube's colour is shaded toward the sky colour by its
  lattice distance from the camera, so depth reads on every preset,
  Beginner included, where the fog never reaches. The shading is one pure
  function with named constants, interpolated into the shader, and is
  computed in the shader from a camera-position uniform (task 139 adds the
  same uniform for its fly-through fade, so whichever task lands second
  reuses it). It adds no per-frame JS work. The nearest layer keeps its
  number contrast, at least 5:1 against `REVEALED_DARK` as
  `js/textures.js` builds it, and only farther layers lose contrast.

**For whatever ships**
- **Where the scene aids draw.** Iterations 2 to 5 live in the renderer,
  which cannot tell a replay from play (`.claude/context/rendering.md`
  § Replay-driven camera). They therefore also draw in the 3D replay viewer
  from its recorded camera, aimed cell and view mode. The readout belongs
  to play only. The end-of-game effects, the end delay and the loss wave
  are unchanged, and while they run the wireframe and the Shift block are
  hidden.
- **Nothing recorded changes.** No action, stat or replay field is added,
  so the replay format, its version and the 3D sampler are unchanged, and
  the recorded games under `tests/fixtures/replays/` still verify.
- **Tests first** (`CLAUDE.md`: full-tdd), per iteration, before its code:
  - A new `tests/readability-3d.test.mjs` pins the pure functions against
    hand-built 3D state views. For iteration 1: the readout's counts for an
    inner, edge and corner cell; hidden neighbours counted as neither;
    chord applies, certain flag, neither, and over-flagged; the text with
    singular and plural flags; and hidden for a closed cell, a flag, a zero
    and a lost game. For iteration 2: the clipped block bounds. For
    iteration 3: the layer of each block cell for views along each axis and
    a diagonal, and 26 drawn positions for an inner cell with hidden
    neighbours. For iteration 4: the dimming curve does not increase and its
    end values. For iteration 5: the shading curve, and the nearest layer's
    contrast floor.
  - A browser test through `__ms` checks that the readout's text shows on
    an aimed number and hides off it, while paused, during the end delay
    and in Classic 2D. `tests/components.test.mjs`'s `MS_HOOK` key list
    stays as it is.
  - A Playwright screenshot of each iteration on each cube preset goes to
    the owner at its checkpoint.
- **What not to break** (`docs/UX_REVIEW.md` § Decisions taken):
  release-to-act with drag-to-cancel; the reference input conventions,
  including picking from the screen centre and the Shift / Space / Ctrl
  view modes, which keep their keys and their pick rules; 3BV, click and
  efficiency accounting; replays recorded by earlier builds; render on
  demand (no frames while nothing moves).
- `docs/ORIGINAL_SPEC.md` keeps describing the original and gains "Web
  port:" notes, in the form of the one on `Esc` / `F5`. They go on the
  Shift and Space lines and in § Drawing rules (per cell), for the
  iterations that shipped: the Shift block's layers and ghost outlines,
  Space's depth dimming, the depth shading, the wireframe and the readout.
- `README.md`'s 3D controls table (the Hold `Shift` and Hold `Space` rows)
  and its § Gameplay fidelity's deliberate changes describe what shipped.
  So do the Shift and Space rows of `js/settings/bindings.js`, and a
  hand-written 3D key list still in the markup when this lands (task 119
  moves those onto the bindings source).
- `.claude/domain/product-design.md` states the agreed design change in
  every passage that stated the old one: the 3D mode keeps its controls
  and feel, and its look gains the readability aids that shipped (the aimed
  number's readout, its neighbourhood outline, the Shift block's layers and

## Decisions
- **Design change agreed (user decision, 2026-10-12):** settled by prior
  instruction. `docs/UX_REVIEW.md` § Approved scope and work plan approves
  P-23 ("Everything numbered is approved except …"; P-23 is not among the
  exceptions) under its condition of attended iterations (row W-18).
  - `product-design.md` § Design decisions, "The 3D mode is the existing 3D
    game", says its look, controls and feel are kept. The look gains the
    readability aids of the iterations the owner accepts. Controls and pick
    rules are unchanged.

  `product-design.md` is normally `/product-design`'s; this task updates it
  for exactly this and for nothing else. A further design decision met at
  implementation time is not covered: leave that passage untouched and name
  it under *Follow-ups* as a precise follow-up.
- **The original spec's Shift and Space drawing rules change on purpose**
  (P-23). `docs/ORIGINAL_SPEC.md` § Drawing rules pins alpha 0.2 for
  numbers under Space and for everything outside the aimed neighbourhood
  under Shift. The spec keeps describing the original and gains "Web port:"
  notes, as tasks 125 to 127 and 139 do. The renderer's context file
  records the new rules for the iterations that ship.
- **One iteration at a time, owner first** (§ Approved scope and work plan's
  condition on P-23). The implementer never builds an iteration before the
  owner has decided on the previous one, and never runs all five in one go.
  The owner's verdict decides each iteration. "Approve" keeps it. "Adjust"
  means rework, then the same checkpoint again. "Drop" means remove it
  entirely, then go on to the next iteration. A dropped iteration is not a
  failed task.
- **The order is the review's** (P-23's "in order of fit with the
  first-person identity"). Iterations 1 and 2 are small, because the counts
  already exist. Iteration 1 is judged from task 125's framed spawn view,
  which is why task 125 comes first.
- **"Closed" means closed and unflagged.** In the review's example
  `2 · 5 closed · 1 flag`, the flag is counted apart from the closed cells.
  With that split, the chord condition (flags = number) and the certain-flag
  condition (closed = number − flags) read straight off the two counts.
- **Readout on numbers only, in play only.** A closed cell, a flag or a
  zero has no neighbourhood to count. The replay viewer is a study tool
  whose redesign is row W-32, so the readout stays out of it. The renderer's
  aids show there anyway, because the renderer cannot tell a replay from
  play.
- **Layers along the axis closest to the view.** "Near, middle and far"
  only has a meaning relative to the camera. Cutting the block along the
  box axis the view is most aligned with keeps the layers as flat lattice
  slices, which the eye can count.
- **Ghost outlines cover every hidden block cell.** The review names hidden
  zeros. Auto-hidden numbers leave the same gap, and the review's goal is
  that the block always shows its 26 positions, so they get the outline
  too.
- **Pure functions carry the tuning.** The curves and the layer rule are
  plain JS functions whose constants are interpolated into the GLSL, so the
  tests pin what the shader draws without a second copy of the numbers.
  The owner's adjustments at a checkpoint change those constants.
- **Boundaries.** Task 119 (W-01) owns the help copy and the one-time aim
  hint. If that hint has landed, the readout and the hint share the space
  beside the crosshair, and neither covers the other. Task 125 (W-14) owns
  the spawn pose and the off-screen arrow on the crosshair ring, and the
  readout leaves room for the arrow. Task 126 (W-19) owns the Ctrl ghosts
  and the solved pulse. Task 127 (W-20) owns the right-click flag-all and
  its undo, and the readout's certain-flag cue only names when that action
  is safe. Task 139 (W-15) owns the fly-through fade and the field of view,
  and the depth shading composes with its fade. Each of these tasks keeps
  the shared alpha table in step, so they can land in any order.
- **Out of scope:** snap-to-cube aiming (I-13) and camera presets (I-12),
  deferred by § Approved scope and work plan; anything changing which cell
  a click acts on, or the 3D rules; the replay viewer as a study tool
  (W-32).

## Manual interventions

⚠ REQUIRES MANUAL INTERVENTION. This task is built in five iterations, and
the owner reviews each one in a running build before the next one starts.
The implementer stops at every checkpoint below. It ends its turn with no
tool call and waits for the owner's verdict: approve, adjust (with what to
change) or drop. It never starts the next iteration on its own. Before each
checkpoint the implementer runs the full test suite green. It serves the
build as the README says (`npx http-server -c-1`). It attaches Playwright
screenshots of the iteration on each cube preset, and it states in two or
three lines what to try. On "adjust" it reworks the iteration and returns
to the same checkpoint. On "drop" it removes the iteration's code, tests
and doc notes, checks the suite is green again, and goes on.

1. After iteration 1 (the readout) passes its tests: the owner plays a few
   3D games from the spawn view and aims at numbers inside and on the edge
   of the box. They judge the readout's place, size, wording and chord /
   certain-flag cues, and approve, adjust or drop it. Verify: the readout's
   browser test passes with the wording the owner settled on.
2. After iteration 2 (the wireframe) passes its tests: the owner aims
   around the box in each view mode and judges whether the outline is
   thin enough not to clutter the view and clear enough to show the block.
   They approve, adjust or drop it. Verify: the block-bounds tests pass and
   a screenshot shows the outline under no mode, Shift, Space and Ctrl.
3. After iteration 3 (the Shift layers and ghost outlines) passes its
   tests: the owner holds Shift on numbers with hidden neighbours, picks
   tint, separation or both, and judges whether all 26 positions read at a
   glance. They approve, adjust or drop it. Verify: the layer and
   26-position tests pass, and a Shift screenshot shows three distinct
   layers and the ghost outlines.
4. After iteration 4 (Space depth dimming) passes its tests: the owner
   holds Space over a column of stacked numbers and judges whether the
   front one is the obvious read. They approve, adjust or drop it. Verify:
   the dimming-curve tests pass with the owner's constants.
5. After iteration 5 (depth shading) passes its tests: the owner plays
   Beginner and Expert cubes and judges whether depth reads without making
   the far layers illegible. They approve, adjust or drop it. Verify: the
   shading-curve and contrast tests pass with the owner's constants. Then
   the docs and context steps run for the iterations that shipped, and the
   task closes.
