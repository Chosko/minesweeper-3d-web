# Task 119 — Mode-aware help and first-use hints

Archived: 2026-10-14
Status: [DONE]

Preconditions: none

Target: claude

## Goal
Make every help surface tell the player about the mode they are actually
playing and the device they are actually holding, and teach first-time
players where they need it: in the first games. This is row W-01 of
`docs/UX_REVIEW.md` § Approved scope and work plan (Phase 1, first; every
later help-related task builds on it). It closes P-01, P-02, P-28, P-31,
P-42 and I-17 of that document. Today the pause card, the main-menu
Controls panel and the H panel all list the 3D keyboard controls, even in
a Classic 2D game (P-01). Nothing tells a newcomer that a 3D number counts
26 cubes or that solved cubes hide (P-02). Nobody learns that Space aims
through numbers (P-28). A no-guess game is invisible once it starts
(P-31). A controller player sees keyboard hints throughout 3D (P-42). Help
lives only behind H and Esc, while the first game is where it is needed
(I-17). The work serves every Classic 2D player who presses Esc, the
first-time 3D player, 3D players with no-guess on, controller-only
players and every new player.

## Acceptance criteria
- **One bindings component** (P-01), built on the bindings source
  (`js/settings/bindings.js`), shows the rows of a given mode (Classic 2D
  or 3D) for a given device. The device is keyboard and mouse, or the
  controller drawn in the connected controller's glyphs. Three places use
  it:
  - the pause card, fed by the active mode and device;
  - the main-menu Controls panel, with Classic 2D and 3D tabs, fed by the
    active device;
  - the in-game H panel, fed by the active mode and device.
  The hard-coded 3D list in the pause card markup and the copy of the 3D
  list in the Controls panel are gone. A row's text is edited in
  `BINDINGS` only.
- The active device is the controller while pad navigation is active (the
  last input came from a connected controller). Otherwise it is keyboard
  and mouse. When the active device changes, every shown surface follows
  it.
- **H and M in both modes** (P-01): in a Classic 2D game, H opens and
  closes the help panel with the Classic 2D rows, and M toggles sound
  through the `muted` setting, as in 3D. The cues 2D will play are W-09's
  (P-62). The HUD strip shows in Classic 2D with only the keys bound there.
  F is not listed because F flags in 2D. The H panel's foot line names
  the release rule only in the mode that has it.
- **Look settings only in 3D** (P-01): the pause card's look sensitivity
  and invert-Y controls show only on a 3D game's pause card. Volume and
  Sound show in both modes.
- **"How 3D works"** (P-02): until the player has finished one 3D game
  (won or lost; an abandoned game does not count), the 3D ready card shows
  a three-line block:
  - a number counts the mines among the 26 cubes around it;
  - empty and solved cubes hide themselves, and holding Ctrl shows them;
  - the first click is always safe.
- **Auto-hide toast** (P-02): the first time auto-hide hides a cube in any
  3D game, a toast says "Solved cubes hide. Hold Ctrl to see them." It
  shows once, and never again on that browser.
- **Space and Shift line** (P-28): the ready card's "Shift/Space/Ctrl view
  modes" line becomes "Hold Space to aim through numbers, Shift to see a
  number's neighbours". The line stays after the rules block goes.
- **One-time aim hint** (P-28): the first time the crosshair rests (about
  one second) on a revealed number with a closed cube behind it on the
  aim ray, a hint says to hold Space to aim through numbers. It shows
  once.
- **Space tap-toggle** (P-28): Space works as a tap-toggle as well as a
  hold. A short tap turns aim-through on until the next tap. A hold works
  exactly as today and ends with the release. Every other view-mode key
  and the controller keep their behaviour. The 3D camera sampler records
  the resulting view mode as it records any view mode.
- **"· no-guess" everywhere it matters** (P-31): on a no-guess game,
  " · no-guess" is appended to the 3D ready card's board line, to the
  overlay bar's board readout and to the pause card. The pause card gains
  a board line naming the board as the readout does. The readout and the
  pause card behave the same way in Classic 2D.
- **First-use hint strips** (I-17): during the player's first three games
  in each mode, a dismissable strip sits at the bottom of the play area.
  - Classic 2D: "Arrows move · Space reveal · F flag · D chord". It
    appears when a key or the controller is first used in the game, and
    names the controller's inputs when the controller is the active
    device.
  - 3D: "Left reveal · Right flag · Hold Space aim through · Hold Shift
    neighbours", shown from the start of play. It replaces the one-word
    "H controls" pill (`#hint-h`) and the pill's `ms3d.hintH.done` flag,
    which is no longer read or written.
  - Dismissing a mode's strip hides it for good in that mode.
- **Pad glyphs while pad navigation is active** (P-42): the HUD strip and
  the hint strip show the controller's glyphs, listing only what the mode
  binds on the controller. The pause card shows the controller rows
  through the bindings component.
- First-use state persists through platform storage. It covers games
  counted per mode, whether a 3D game was finished, whether the toast and
  the aim hint were shown, and the strip dismissals. Storage failure never
  blocks play: the hints then behave as on first use for that session.
- None of the H panel, the HUD strip, the hint strips, the toast and the
  aim hint shows in the replay viewer.
- Tests (full TDD) cover:
  - the bindings component per mode × device with glyphs;
  - the active-device switch;
  - the pause card's mode-dependent controls and look settings;
  - the Controls panel's tabs;
  - H and M in Classic 2D;
  - the ready card's block and its disappearance after one finished 3D
    game, but not after an abandoned one;
  - the one-time toast and aim hint;
  - Space tap versus hold;
  - " · no-guess" on all three surfaces;
  - the strips' three-game window, first-key trigger in 2D, dismissal and
    pad glyphs;
  - the first-use document's load, save, seeding and failure fallback.

  The existing suite stays green.
- `.claude/domain/features/settings.md` states the agreed design change:
  the pause card keeps quick access to volume in both modes, and to look
  sensitivity and invert Y on a 3D game's pause card only. It says this
  in every passage that stated the old shortcuts. Nothing else in the
  document changes.
- The context files describing the changed surfaces state the new state,
  per `CLAUDE.md` § Editing Discipline: `app-shell.md`, `settings.md`
  (§ Bindings are one table) and `input.md` for the Space toggle, plus
  `testing.md` for new test files.

## Decisions
- **Design change agreed (director decision, `docs/UX_REVIEW.md` P-01
  proposed solution, approved under § Approved scope and work plan,
  recorded 2026-10-12):** `settings.md` § Architecture "Pause-card
  shortcuts" says the pause card keeps sensitivity, invert Y and volume.
  The look settings now show only on a 3D game's pause card. Settled
  here: the bindings list and the in-game help keep reading one source.
  `.claude/domain/features/settings.md` is normally `/architect`'s; this
  task updates it for exactly this and for nothing else. A further design
  decision met at implementation time is not covered: leave that passage
  untouched and name it under *Follow-ups* as a precise follow-up.
- **P-28 is closed in full** (ready-card line, one-time aim hint, Space
  tap-toggle), because W-01 is the only row that closes it. The tap-toggle
  is Space only, as P-28 states; the controller's hold button is
  unchanged.
- **First-use state lives in one platform-storage document** (for example
  `shell.firstUse`, beside `shell.lastMode`), not in new `ms3d.*` keys.
  The records store's counters count an abandoned game as a loss and can
  fail to save, so they are not the source. When the document is first
  created, it is seeded from the records store's per-mode game counters:
  a returning player's recorded games count toward the three, and any
  recorded 3D game counts as the finished one, so a returning player is
  not taught again.
- **A game counts toward the three** when the mode reports `game
  started`.
- **The hint strip and HUD strip draw key and button names from the
  bindings source**, so the later `input-rebinding` feature reaches them
  through the source it replaces.
- **" · no-guess" also applies in Classic 2D** on the readout and pause
  card. P-31 names the three surfaces without limiting them to 3D, and
  both modes share the readout and the pause card.
- **Out of scope:**
  - the dark-theme Controls panel colours and its contrast test: P-03,
    W-02;
  - 2D sound cues: P-62, W-09;
  - the "3D needs a mouse or a controller" ready-card line and keyboard
    3D controls: P-40, W-24;
  - help copy for right-click on a number: P-21, W-20;
  - the "a number disappears once it is solved" help line and the solved
    pulse: P-35, W-19;
  - focus containment in the Controls panel and pause card: P-43, W-26;
  - "Esc: Back" labels: P-07, W-05;
  - Sound wording: P-10, W-07;
  - the replay viewer's own key hints: P-52, W-32;
  - pad-aware Records: I-18, W-25;
  - the Learn 3D board: I-01, W-21, which builds on this task;
  - rebinding: P-44, which waits for `input-rebinding`.

  Keep the review's "What not to break" list intact: release-to-act, the
  reference input conventions and the confirmations.
