# Task 140 — Look at the board and end delay

Target: claude

## Goal
Let a 3D player look at the board they just finished, and let the loss wave
finish before the results card shows. This is row W-16 of
`docs/UX_REVIEW.md` § Approved scope and work plan (Area: 3D, results; Size:
Medium; Phase 3). It closes P-22 of that document (Major; learners and
enthusiasts doing a post-mortem), approved by decision 4 of § Decisions
taken ("A player-chosen Look at the board after a 3D game? Yes, free flight
over the frozen board — P-22 proceeds"). P-22's problem: after the end
effect the 3D results card takes the whole window and the board, the
exploded cube, the mines and the wrong flags are gone with no way back
(Watch replay then End shows the board from the recorded camera, two clicks
later, and nothing says so), while in 2D the revealed board stays behind the
card; and the flat 1 s end delay cuts the loss wave short on large boards.
Its approved solution: a "Look at the board" action on the 3D results card
(and Esc) hides the card and the player flies over the frozen, fully
revealed board with the exploded cube and wrong flags marked, read-only,
until Esc or any button brings the card back; the scene stays dimmed behind
the card meanwhile; the end delay scales to the board's diagonal, capped at
2.5 s.

## Acceptance criteria
- **End delay scales to the diagonal** (P-22): the 3D end delay is one pure
  function of the board's dimensions, exported beside `END_DELAY_MS` in
  `js/shell/mode-3d.js`, shared by win and loss: it grows with the board's
  diagonal so that on a loss the reveal wave from the exploded cube reaches
  the cell farthest from it, and that cell's reveal animation completes,
  before the delay ends; it never exceeds 2500 ms and never falls below the
  current 1000 ms. The renderer's wave pacing (`_waveSetup`'s per-cell
  delay) is derived from the same function, so the wave and the delay
  cannot drift apart. The adapter runs the delay for the finished board's
  dimensions on `flow.clock` exactly as it runs `END_DELAY_MS` today. Task
  137's rule that any click, key or controller button during the delay
  ends it is not re-implemented here; where task 137 has landed it applies
  unchanged to the longer delay.
- **Look at the board** (P-22, decision 4): the results card of a 3D game
  carries a *Look at the board* action (a new kit button in the results
  view, shown only for a 3D game's summary, after the existing actions'
  primary *Play again*, which keeps default focus). Activating it, or a Back
  input on that card (Esc, and the controller's back buttons, which the
  shell treats as the same Back), hides the card and opens the look: the
  player flies over the finished board with the 3D flight controls — mouse
  look and keyboard flight, or the controller's sticks — starting from the
  camera pose the game ended with. The Back to menu button keeps its
  meaning; on a Classic 2D results card nothing changes.
- **The look is read-only**: no board action (reveal, flag, chord), no
  pause offered, no timer running, nothing recorded — the game's records,
  history, its sealed replay and the replay library are byte-for-byte what
  they were when the card first showed. The view-mode keys (`Shift`,
  `Space`, `Ctrl`) and spacing work as in play, as viewing aids. The board
  is drawn in its end state: on a loss every mine shown with the exploded
  cube marked and wrong flags crossed, the board revealed as the engine left
  it; on a win the solved board with unflagged mines shown as flags. That
  end state is drawn the same after a Watch replay and Back to the card
  (the renderer's wrong-flag marking survives the replay viewer handing the
  game back, e.g. through `setWrongFlags`).
- **Back to the card**: `Esc`, any mouse button, or any controller button
  ends the look and shows the results card again with the same summary and
  comparison, focus on *Play again*; the press that ends the look does
  nothing else, and its release, a key's auto-repeat or a held button do not
  activate anything on the card. In pointer-locked look the browser's
  release of the lock on `Esc` returns to the card (it does not pause), and
  that same `Esc` does not reopen the look. A one-line hint on the look
  names the way back.
- **Pointer lock**: activating *Look at the board* with a pointer or the
  keyboard is the pointer-lock gesture; with a controller the look is
  lockless. When lock is refused or fails (the browser's re-lock cooldown
  included) the card stays shown with a one-line note, and a fresh press
  tries again.
- **Scene dimmed behind the card** (P-22): over a 3D game the results card
  shows on a dimmed translucent surface with the 3D scene drawn behind it,
  instead of the opaque takeover surface, in both themes; the card's text
  keeps the contrast the existing contrast checks require. `shouldRender`
  draws the 3D scene under a 3D results screen on demand (resize, an end
  effect still running, the first frame after the card shows) and draws no
  frame when nothing changed.
- **Tests first**: `tests/shell-mode-3d.test.mjs` (or
  `tests/mode3d-end.test.mjs`) pins the end-delay function — 1000 ms on cube
  Beginner and small boards, growing with the diagonal, 2500 ms at 100³ and
  never above — and the adapter reporting finished after that delay on the
  fake clock, not after `END_DELAY_MS`. `tests/results.test.mjs` pins the
  Look action shown for a 3D summary only, its callback, and Back on a 3D
  results card opening the look while Back to menu still goes to the menu.
  `tests/mode3d-end.test.mjs` replaces its "no frame is drawn under a 3D
  game's results screen" check with the scene drawn behind it, and adds
  static checks that the look gates every board action and the pause. A
  Playwright run through `__ms`, with SwiftShader, checks: a 3D loss on a
  large board whose wave reaches the far side before the card shows; the
  card over the dimmed scene; Look at the board, flying, a click doing no
  board action, `Esc` bringing the card back; a win's solved board looked at
  the same way; the records and the replay unchanged after a look.
- **What not to break** (`docs/UX_REVIEW.md` § Decisions taken):
  release-to-act with drag-to-cancel; the reference input conventions; 3BV,
  click and efficiency accounting; the three-way best comparison;
  auto-pinned best replays; the confirmations that say "It will count as a
  loss". Records and replays are untouched.
- The owned documents state the agreed design change — a 3D game's end
  delay scales to the board's diagonal, capped at 2.5 s, so the loss wave
  reaches the far side; the 3D results card shows over the dimmed 3D scene;
  a *Look at the board* action (and Back on the card) opens a read-only free
  flight over the frozen finished board until `Esc` or any button brings the
  card back — in every passage that stated the old one, and settle the
  points listed under Decisions:
  `.claude/domain/features/3d-results-records-screens.md`,
  `.claude/domain/features/results-screen.md`,
  `.claude/domain/features/3d-board-graph.md` and
  `.claude/domain/product-design.md`. Nothing else in those documents
  changes.

## Decisions
- **Design change agreed (user decision, 2026-10-12):** settled by prior
  instruction — P-22 of `docs/UX_REVIEW.md` and decision 4 of its §
  Decisions taken, approved under § Approved scope and work plan (row W-16).
  - `3d-results-records-screens.md` — the opening paragraph ("the same
    results screen as Classic 2D takes over completely"), § Scope and
    non-goals ("The results screen covering the 3D scene completely; the end
    banner and flying around the finished board are removed", and the
    rejected non-goal "A results panel the player can close to fly around
    the finished board"), § Architecture, "3D end sequence" ("After a fixed
    end delay of about one second … The delay is one constant shared by win
    and loss") and "Results takeover" ("on an opaque surface … the 3D scene
    is not visible behind it and stops rendering while it shows"), and §
    Interfaces and contracts (a new *Look at the board* contract; Back on a
    3D results card): the scene is drawn dimmed behind the card, the
    player can choose a read-only free flight over the frozen board, and
    the end delay is a function of the board's diagonal shared by win and
    loss, capped at 2.5 s. "Banner retirement"'s "a 3D game is never
    returned to" still holds: the look is a view of the finished board, not
    a return to the game.
  - `results-screen.md` § Interfaces and contracts, "Board shown behind"
    ("Over 3D the screen is opaque and takes over"): over 3D the finished
    board is drawn dimmed behind the screen, and the 3D screen carries the
    *Look at the board* action.
  - `3d-board-graph.md` § "No action after the game ends" ("The results
    screen takes over once a 3D game ends, so the player cannot reach the
    board then anyway"): the player can look at the finished board but take
    no action on it.
  - `product-design.md` § 3D ("the finished board is not flown around
    afterwards"): the player can choose to fly over the frozen finished
    board from the results screen.

  Settled here, without conflict: the open question at the end of
  `3d-results-records-screens.md` (the end delay's length — grow with the
  board, capped at 2.5 s, floor 1000 ms) is answered and removed. These
  documents are normally `/architect`'s (`product-design.md`
  `/product-design`'s); this task updates them for exactly this and for
  nothing else. A further design decision met at implementation time is not
  covered: leave that passage untouched and name it under *Follow-ups* as a
  precise follow-up.
- **"Any button" is taken as written**: `Esc`, any mouse button and any
  controller button bring the card back. In the look a controller flies
  with the sticks alone (its A/B up and down, bumpers and Y view modes are
  buttons and so return the card); with task 139's forward-follows-the-view
  (P-26) the sticks reach any height. The keyboard keeps the flight and
  view-mode keys, which are not buttons in P-22's sense.
- **Back on the card is the look**: P-22 says "and Esc"; the shell treats
  `Esc` and the controller's back buttons as one Back input, so both open
  the look on a 3D results card. Back to menu stays one button away.
- **"Fully revealed" is the engine's end state**: the board as the loss (or
  the win) left it — every mine shown, the board revealed, the exploded cube
  and wrong flags marked, per `3d-board-graph.md` § "On a loss". No extra
  reveal is computed for the look.
- **The delay keeps its 1000 ms floor and is shared by win and loss**: small
  boards are unchanged, and the design's one-delay-for-both shape holds; the
  win confetti on a large board gets the same longer delay.
- **Lock refused keeps the card**: a lockless look with a mouse has no mouse
  look, so a refused lock leaves the card up with a note rather than opening
  a crippled look; a controller's look is lockless as its play is.
- **Out of scope**: the 2D results screen and its blurred board; task 137's
  input that cuts the end delay short (row W-11), which this task only
  inherits; listing the new action and its `Esc` in the help, which is task
  119's (row W-01) — it lists it if it needs a key; the results card's
  hierarchy (task 128, row W-27); flight changes (task 139, row W-15); the
  replay viewer and Records.
- **Boundaries**: tasks 137, 128, 130, 133 and 139 also touch
  `js/main.js`, the results view or `3d-results-records-screens.md`. None
  depends on this task nor this on them; whichever lands second merges
  around the first.
