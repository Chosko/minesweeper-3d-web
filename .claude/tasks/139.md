# Task 139 — 3D flight: fly-through fade, forward follows the view, spacing, FOV

Target: claude

## Goal
Make flying through the 3D box comfortable and predictable: cubes the camera
reaches turn to glass instead of filling the screen, W goes where the player
looks, the spacing wheel reaches useful spacings in a few notches with the
camera keeping its framing, and the field of view becomes a setting. This is
row W-15 of `docs/UX_REVIEW.md` § Approved scope and work plan (Area: 3D,
Size: Medium, Phase 3). It closes P-25, P-26, P-30 and P-32 of that
document, under its decision 5 and the conditions of § Approved scope and
work plan ("P-25 takes the fade, not the half-speed alternative; P-26
changes the forward vector with no new setting and leaves Q and E as world
up and down"). Today, at 50 units/s, holding W from spawn is inside a cube in
under two seconds: the view becomes a window-sized number on a pink field,
and picking still works from inside, so a click acts on a cube the player
cannot identify (P-25, Major, a first-time player's first W press and anyone
approaching a cube). W does not go where you look: the forward vector keeps
full horizontal speed at any pitch, an original quirk, so looking straight
down and pressing W moves as much forward as down, the aimed cube slides
away while flying at it, and reaching a lower layer needs Q (P-26, Major,
everyone). The spacing wheel moves 0.04 per notch (23 notches to reach 2.0),
its readout chip fades after about a second, and the camera stays put, so
at spacing 2 the box spills off the view and at 4 the camera is inside the
exploded board (P-30, Minor, every 3D player). The field of view is a fixed
45° vertical, narrow for first person at 50 units/s (P-32, Minor,
motion-sensitive players). The work serves every 3D player, first-time
players most (P-25, P-26), and motion-sensitive players (P-32).

## Acceptance criteria
- **Fly through glass** (P-25, § Approved scope: the fade, not half speed,
  no collision): every drawn cube whose centre lies within the fly-through
  distance of the camera is drawn at alpha 0.25 (a named constant inside the
  review's 20 to 30 %), whatever its kind and whatever Shift, Space and Ctrl
  do; with Shift or Space also fading it, the lower alpha wins. The
  fly-through distance is a named constant equal to 1.5 pitches at the start
  spacing (`1.5 * 4 * SPACING_START`, 6.6 world units). Beyond it, the alpha
  ramps linearly back to 1 over a named band of about half a pitch at the
  start spacing, so a cube fades in rather than pops; cubes in the band stay
  pickable. The fade runs on the Shift fade's path: the vertex shader's
  alpha table decides it from a camera-position uniform, the opaque pass
  drops those cubes and the transparent pass draws them back to front with
  the existing sort. With neither Shift nor Space held, the transparent list
  holds only the cubes within the fade band, built from the lattice cells
  around the camera, so no per-frame O(n) JavaScript work is added; the
  renderer still re-sorts only when the camera moved or a list is dirty.
- **Unpickable within the distance** (P-25): `pickCell` and `pickCellBrute`
  skip every cell whose centre lies within the fly-through distance of the
  ray origin, and still agree with each other (the debug hook's `pickWith`
  cross-check). The ray continues past a skipped cell, so from inside the
  box the crosshair selects the first cube beyond the glass. The rule
  applies wherever picking runs: play and the 3D replay viewer's aimed cell.
- **Forward follows the view** (P-26, decision 5: fixed outright, no new
  setting): `FlyCamera.move` and `moveAxes` use the true forward vector
  `(sin(-yaw)·cos(pitch), sin(pitch), cos(-yaw)·cos(pitch))`; W moves along
  the view direction, S against it, at `MOVE_SPEED`. Looking straight down,
  W moves straight down with no horizontal component; at pitch 0 movement
  is unchanged from today. A and D stay horizontal strafes; Q, E and the
  controller's up axis stay world down and up. The controller's left stick
  uses the same vectors (`moveAxes` matches `move` key for key, speed still
  capped at `MOVE_SPEED`). No setting is added and the quirk is not kept
  anywhere.
- **Spacing step and acceleration** (P-30): one isolated wheel notch changes
  the spacing by exactly 0.1; notches arriving in quick succession step
  further, growing up to a named cap per notch, and the step falls back to
  0.1 after a short pause. The acceleration is timed on event arrival, so a
  trackpad's many fractional notches scale proportionally and do not run
  away. Wheel up still means larger, the range stays [1, 10], and the
  computation is one pure function shared by the wheel, the controller's
  D-pad (one 0.1 step per press and per auto-repeat) and the debug hook's
  `wheel`.
- **Spacing readout** (P-30): the 3D tools strip shows the spacing for the
  whole of 3D play, as an entry like its other key hints —
  `X` spacing 1.1 — to one decimal, updated on every change (wheel, D-pad,
  `X`, the reset to 1.1 at a new game). The fading spacing chip
  (`#chip-spacing`, `ui.showSpacing`'s toast) is gone. The strip stays out
  of Classic 2D.
- **Spacing toggle key** (P-30): during 3D play (the board accepts input,
  before the first click included; never while paused or during the end
  delay), `X` sets the spacing to 2.0, or to 1.1 when it is already 2.0 to
  the readout's precision. It does not repeat while held and records no
  action. `X` is the key this task adds; task 119 (W-01) lists it in the
  help surfaces through the bindings source.
- **Camera distance follows the spacing** (P-30): on every spacing change,
  from wheel, D-pad or `X`, the camera position is scaled by the ratio of
  the new spacing to the old about the board centre, and yaw and pitch are
  kept. The camera therefore keeps its place relative to the cells: the
  cube under the crosshair stays under it (unless it comes within the
  fly-through distance), the box keeps its framing, and the 3D camera
  sampler's cell-unit sample does not change, so a spacing change adds no
  movement sample.
- **Field of view setting** (P-32, decision 5: the slider stays as
  proposed): the schema gains `fieldOfView`, a number from 45 to 90 in steps
  of 1, default 60, shown in Settings § Graphics as a slider labelled
  "Field of view" with a degree format (`60°`). An applier owns the 3D
  camera's vertical field of view: it sets the renderer's camera `fov`,
  updates its projection, and the next frame redraws; the change applies at
  once, in play, in the menu backdrop and in the 3D replay viewer. Task 125
  (W-14) reads the current field of view for the spawn framing; this task
  does not change the spawn.
- **Existing installs keep 45°** (P-32, "default 60 for new installs"): a
  stored settings document without `fieldOfView` loads it as 45, and so
  does a first start that carries over at least one legacy `ms3d.*` value;
  only an install with neither (a new install), or one whose storage is
  unreadable, corrupt or newer, gets 60. The value is written into the
  document by the next save as any other value is. `SETTINGS_VERSION` stays
  1, so `js/theme.js`'s pre-paint read is untouched.
- **Replays unaffected**: replays record camera positions, not inputs, so
  replays recorded before this change play back their recorded camera
  unchanged, and the recorded games under `tests/fixtures/replays/` still
  verify. The replay format, its version and the 3D sampler's format are
  unchanged.
- **Bindings and README**: the 3D keyboard rows of `js/settings/bindings.js`
  gain `[X]` "Toggle spacing 1.1 / 2.0", and the `[W][A][S][D]` and Wheel
  rows say that forward follows the view and that the wheel changes the
  spacing with the camera; a hand-written 3D key list still in the markup
  when this lands (the pause card's and the controls modal's, which task 119
  moves onto the bindings source) gets the same rows. The README's 3D
  controls table and controller table follow, and its § Gameplay fidelity
  drops "camera movement (including its quirks)" from what is kept and
  lists fly through glass, forward follows the view, the spacing step,
  toggle and camera scaling, and the field of view among the deliberate
  changes.
- **Tests first**: a new `tests/camera-flight.test.mjs` pins:
  - W for one second at pitches −90°, −45°, 0°, 30°, 90° and several yaws
    moves exactly `MOVE_SPEED` along the view direction (straight down at
    90°); Q and E move only on Y; A and D only on XZ;
  - the spacing step: one notch 0.1, a fast run accelerating to the cap, the
    reset after a pause, fractional notches proportional, the clamp to
    [1, 10]; the `X` toggle from 1.1, 2.0 and another value;
  - the camera scaling: the cell-unit position (`cameraSample`) is unchanged
    across a spacing change and the cube under the crosshair stays picked;
  - picking skips cells within the fly-through distance, picks the first cube
    beyond, and `pickCell` agrees with `pickCellBrute` on rays starting
    inside the box.
  `tests/gamepad.test.mjs` keeps `moveAxes` equal to `move`. The settings
  tests pin the `fieldOfView` schema entry, the 60 default for a new install,
  45 for a stored document without the key and for a legacy carry-over, the
  applier setting the camera's `fov` and projection, the Graphics slider,
  and the new bindings row. A Playwright check flies into a cube on each
  cube preset (glass, crosshair selecting beyond), spins the wheel, presses
  `X`, and moves the field of view slider, checked by eye.
- **What not to break** (`docs/UX_REVIEW.md` § Decisions taken):
  release-to-act with drag-to-cancel; the reference input conventions —
  0.2°/px look, pitch clamp, 50 u/s, picking from the screen centre, the
  Shift / Space / Ctrl view modes and their alphas; 3BV, click and
  efficiency accounting; replays recorded by earlier builds; render on
  demand (no frames while nothing moves).
- `docs/ORIGINAL_SPEC.md` keeps describing the original and gains
  "Web port:" notes, in the form of the one on `Esc` / `F5`: under
  § Controls, on the forward-vector quirk line (the port's W follows the
  view; Q and E stay world down and up) and on picking (cubes within 1.5
  pitches of the camera fade and cannot be picked); under § Grid & geometry,
  on the wheel line (0.1 per notch with acceleration, `X` toggling 1.1 and
  2.0, the camera scaling with the spacing).
- `.claude/domain/product-design.md` states the agreed design change — the
  3D mode keeps its controls and feel except that forward follows the view,
  cubes near the camera fade to glass and cannot be picked, the spacing
  steps 0.1 with acceleration, has a toggle key and carries the camera with
  it, and the field of view is a setting — in every passage that stated the
  old one. Nothing else in the document changes.
- `.claude/domain/features/settings.md` states the agreed design change — a
  field of view setting (45 to 90°, default 60) in Graphics, applied to the
  3D camera, and a stored document from before it, or a legacy carry-over,
  loading it as 45 instead of the default — in every passage that stated the
  old one (§ Scope's Graphics item, § Architecture's settings store and
  appliers, § Data and state's saved settings). Nothing else in the
  document changes.

## Decisions
- **Design change agreed (user decision, 2026-10-12):** settled by prior
  instruction — `docs/UX_REVIEW.md` decision 5 (P-26 fixed outright with no
  new setting, Q and E world up and down; the FOV slider P-32 as proposed),
  § Approved scope and work plan (P-25 takes the fade, not the half-speed
  alternative) and P-30, approved without condition (row W-15).
  - `product-design.md` § Design decisions, "The 3D mode is the existing 3D
    game", says its look, controls and feel are kept; forward now follows
    the view, near cubes fade and cannot be picked, the spacing control
    changes, and the field of view becomes a setting.
  - `features/settings.md` § Scope, § Architecture and § Data and state list
    the settings and say the store fills a missing value with its default;
    the task adds the field of view setting and loads it as 45 for an
    existing install (P-32's "default 60 for new installs").

  `product-design.md` is normally `/product-design`'s and
  `features/settings.md` `/architect`'s; this task updates them for exactly
  this and for nothing else. A further design decision met at
  implementation time is not covered: leave that passage untouched and name
  it under *Follow-ups* as a precise follow-up.
- **The original spec's quirk and wheel step are changed on purpose**
  (decision 5, P-30). `docs/ORIGINAL_SPEC.md` says of the quirk "Keep it."
  and pins 0.04 per notch; the spec keeps describing the original and gains
  "Web port:" notes, as tasks 125 to 127 do. The context layer's
  preserved-controls list drops the forward-vector quirk and the 0.04 step.
- **Fade distance fixed at the start spacing.** "About 1.5 pitches" is
  taken at the start spacing (6.6 world units) and not scaled with the
  spacing: at wide spacings a pitch-relative radius would fade and block
  neighbours dozens of units away, while the problem it solves is a cube
  the size of the screen. The ramp band past the distance is for the eye
  only; the pick rule is the distance itself.
- **Existing installs keep 45°.** P-32 sets 60 "for new installs"; a player
  who already has settings, or legacy values to carry over, has been
  playing at 45 and keeps it until they move the slider. It is done at load,
  not by a document version bump, which would break the theme script's
  version 1 read.
- **`X` for the toggle.** Free in both modes' bindings and in the keys tasks
  125 (`Home`, `R`) and 130 (`F2`) claim; D and F are taken in 3D (decision
  12). It sits under the left hand beside WASD. W-24 (keyboard-only 3D,
  free keys only) must treat `X` as taken.
- **Toggle rule.** `X` goes to 2.0 from any spacing other than 2.0, and back
  to 1.1 from 2.0, so it is one predictable two-state switch; the change is
  instant, and the camera scales with it as with the wheel.
- **Scaling about the board centre.** Scaling the camera's position with
  the spacing keeps it at the same cell-unit place, which is what keeps the
  framing, the aimed cube and the replay sampler's samples unchanged; a far
  camera moves farther, as its cells do.
- **No controller toggle.** P-30 names a key; the controller keeps the
  D-pad at the new step. Controller and touch layouts belong to W-23 and
  W-25.
- **Boundary with task 119** (W-01): 119 owns the help surfaces and their
  copy; this task adds its rows to the bindings source and, while the
  hand-written lists exist, to them too, so either task can land first.
- **Boundary with task 125** (W-14): 125 owns the spawn pose and reads the
  current field of view; this task owns the field of view setting and
  leaves the spawn alone. Either can land first.
- **Out of scope:** the spawn pose and world reference (P-24, P-27, task
  125); the help copy for the new key (task 119); the half-speed alternative
  and a hard collision (P-25); a "Fly where you look" setting (P-26, ruled
  out by decision 5); camera presets and snap aiming (I-12, I-13, deferred);
  touch controls for 3D (W-23, after this task); keyboard-only 3D (W-24).
