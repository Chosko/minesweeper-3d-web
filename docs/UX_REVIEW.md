# Minesweeper 3D UX Review

Oct 11, 2026 · @Chosko

A game-director review of the web build at its current state, from the player's side only: first run, board choice, Classic 2D and 3D play, every input method, results, records, replays and settings. Problems are numbered P-nn and improvements I-nn so each can become a task.

## At a glance

The core game is trustworthy and the shell around it is not yet teaching, scaling or rewarding the player. Five reviewers (first-run UX, game design, first-person 3D, input and accessibility, competitive enthusiast) played about 130 games in headless Chromium at 1024×768 to 1920×1080, on emulated phones and tablets, with mouse, keyboard, a faked controller and touch, in both themes. Rules, input conventions, 3BV, click counts, efficiency, no-guess separation, records and replays all matched hand computation and Minesweeper Online conventions. Not observed: sound, real frame rates, hardware controllers and touch screens, fullscreen; timings quoted from the browser are upper bounds.

Five themes carry most of the weight:

1. **The 3D on-ramp is missing.** A standard first click opens nothing half the time, Cube Beginner is either a coin flip or solved by one click, the spawn view cuts the box in half, nobody is told that numbers count 26 cubes or that solved cubes hide, and the lost board vanishes after one second.
2. **Help is mode-blind.** The pause card, the Controls panel and the H key explain 3D to Classic 2D players; the dark-theme Controls panel is unreadable.
3. **Whole input classes are locked out.** Touch cannot flag or pan in 2D and gets trapped in 3D; a keyboard alone cannot play 3D; a controller skips controls in the custom form and Records.
4. **Enthusiast precision is one notch short.** Times in tenths, pause stops the clock silently, no click counts or rank, Expert scrolls on a laptop, a browser crash turns a win into a loss.
5. **Nothing calls the player back.** The menu knows only "Last played"; no daily board, no board codes, no local top-10, no next target.

## First run and main menu

The path to a game is short (two clicks to a 2D board, three to 3D play) and the confirmations, validation copy and last-choice memory are right. What fails is what the shell tells the player: help, defaults and copy all assume the 3D game.

## Classic 2D play

Classic 2D is the reference it promises to be: flag on right press, reveal and chord on release, left+right and 1.5-click chords, middle button, release-off-board cancel, drag-to-cell, press feedback, keyboard cursor with auto-repeat, and 3BV, clicks and efficiency that matched hand computation in every game. The problems are around the board, not in it.

## 3D play

The 3D game is sound underneath: release-to-act with drag-to-cancel, a readable aimed-cube outline, 26 distinct numbers, auto-hide that only ever hides a correctly solved number, instant generation on the presets, and clear end effects. The experience around it is tuned for the 2011 original's author, not for a player meeting it for the first time. The measured figures below come from the game's own generator and solver over 100 seeds per preset.

## Input methods

Mouse, keyboard and controller are each done thoroughly where they are supported: every screen is keyboard-operable with correct focus restore and a consistent Esc; the controller has a dead zone, release semantics with press feedback, held-button suppression across screen changes, disconnect pause and PlayStation glyphs; the mouse is faithful. The gaps are the combinations nobody designed for.

## Results, records and replays
